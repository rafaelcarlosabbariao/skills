# Visual forms and the evidence grammar

Checked by web search, 2026-11-18. This file decides which form; build the chart with your charting tool.

## The evidence grammar

Drawn from published trend-report decks (the reports named below) and from charts that failed a reader because
they decorated a number without showing anything about it.

1. **Compared to what?** Attributed to Tufte's *Envisioning Information* (1990) by secondary sources: "At the heart
   of quantitative reasoning is a single question: Compared to what?" A number with no comparator is decoration.
2. **Because of what?** The theory sits on the chart it explains: Rogers' curve shaded under the group studied
   (Highsnobiety and BCG, *Culture Culture Culture*).
3. **The base on the slide:** n, dates, source; the survey question or the prompt shown word for word (Highsnobiety
   and BCG, *Navigating What's Next*).
4. **The sample before the findings:** nested shapes (Artsy 2021: 2,290 surveyed, 765 collectors, 139 next-gen);
   the method as numbered stages, each with its count (Gharage, *Airport Futures*; Morning, *The State of Social*).
5. **Change as a path:** slopes or linked bars, never two big numbers side by side.
6. **Outside voices only beside data.** No slide whose argument is a quote of the author.
7. **Every visual shows a relationship, a change or a mechanism** a reader could not see in the table.

## Forms by job

### Change over time
- **Slope chart / linked bars** for two points; **annotated line** with the turning points labeled; **small
  multiples** for many series on one scale.
- Tufte: small multiples are scanned quickly once the reader learns the format; sparklines are word-sized graphics
  set in the text (*Beautiful Evidence*, 2006).
- Exemplars: Ashkenas and Parlapiano, "How the Recession Reshaped the Economy, in 255 Charts" (NYT Upshot, June
  2014); Rosling, "200 Countries, 200 Years, 4 Minutes" (BBC Four, *The Joy of Stats*, 2010), life expectancy
  against income since 1810.

### Comparison
- **Dot plot** or **linked bars** on one shared scale; small multiples. Never two y-axes.
- Exemplar: Hanah Anderson and Matt Daniels, "Film Dialogue from 2,000 screenplays, Broken Down by Gender and Age"
  (The Pudding, 2016): women had the most dialogue in 22% of films; the authors state the screenplay limitation.

### Scale or magnitude
- **Unit charts** and **icon arrays** in a familiar unit; a **true-scale scroll** for extremes.
- Exemplar: Matt Korostoff, 1-pixel wealth ("Wealth, shown to scale"), one pixel per unit of money.
- Warning: Nightingale's 1858 rose plots data to the radius, so outer areas read larger than they are.

### Distribution or inequality
- **Strip or beeswarm**, **histogram**, **percentile line**; label the tails.
- Exemplar: NYT Upshot, "Some Colleges Have More Students From the Top 1 Percent Than the Bottom 60" (18 January
  2017, from Chetty and colleagues): 38 colleges; top 1% above $630,000 a year against the bottom 60% below $65,000.

### Part-to-whole, risk, the sample
- **Icon arrays** and **waffles**; **nested areas** for a sample.
- Galesic, Garcia-Retamero and Gigerenzer (2009): icon arrays raised accuracy for low- and high-numeracy readers;
  a 2013 review found visual aids confused some older adults low in numeracy and graph literacy.

### Cause or mechanism
- **Rule out the rivals:** each candidate cause drawn against the observed, in sequence, until one fits. Exemplar:
  Roston and Migliozzi, "What's Really Warming the World?" (Bloomberg, 2015), from NASA GISS model data; critics
  noted it plots modeled responses, so name the model.
- **Cause and effect on one plane:** John Snow's 1854 Broad Street map, deaths as bars around one pump, with the
  exceptions checked (Tufte, *Visual Explanations*, 1997). The epidemic was already waning when the handle came off;
  say what the chart cannot prove.

### Hidden system or rule set
- **Explorable explanation:** the reader changes a rule and watches the output. Bret Victor, "Explorable
  Explanations" (10 March 2011): guide the reader's attention to the phenomenon. Exemplar: Vi Hart and Nicky Case,
  *Parable of the Polygons* (December 2014, public domain), after Schelling's "Dynamic Models of Segregation"
  (1971); it adds an anti-bias setting that reverses the outcome.
- **Stepped diagram** of local rules, interactions, the collective output.
- Warning: a simulation shows how a rule could produce an outcome; label it a model, beside the measured data.

### Network or flow
- **Flow map** or **Sankey** with totals; **two-mode network** (people by groups).
- Exemplars: Charles Minard, Napoleon's Russian campaign (20 November 1869), six variables on one plane, which
  Tufte called possibly "the best statistical graphic ever drawn" (1983); Kieran Healy, "Using Metadata to Find Paul
  Revere" (2013), a people-by-groups table turned into two networks.

### Surprise against expectation
- **Predict, then reveal:** the reader draws or picks a guess, then sees the data and other readers' guesses.
- Exemplar: Aisch, Cox and Quealy, "You Draw It" (NYT Upshot, May 2015), college attendance by family income; the
  real line is close to straight.

### Uncertainty
- **Hypothetical outcome plots** (Hullman, Resnick and Adar, *PLOS ONE* 10(11), 2015): animated draws beat error bars
  and violin plots for judging which of two or three quantities is larger.
- The NYT election needle (2016): an 85% chance was read as certainty; Nate Cohn: "We failed at explaining that an 85
  percent chance is not 100 percent." Dropped in 2020 for per-state needles.

### Precedent
- **Paired timeline**, the old record redrawn beside the new on one scale.
- Exemplar: W. E. B. Du Bois and Atlanta University students, data portraits for the 1900 Paris Exposition (sources
  say 60 or 63 charts), on Black life in Georgia; collected in Battle-Baptiste and Rusert, *W. E. B. Du Bois's Data
  Portraits*.

### One room standing for many
- The room or object marked as one dot inside the distribution it belongs to, with the count beside it.
- Exemplar of the long-form frame: John Branch, "Snow Fall" (NYT, 20 December 2012; Pulitzer for feature writing,
  2013), narrative with interactive graphics and the science woven in.

## Data humanism (Giorgia Lupi)

"Data Humanism" (Print, dated fall 2016 or 30 January 2017 by different sources): small data, context and
imperfection set against big-data abstraction; *Dear Data* with Stefanie Posavec (2014 to 2015, book 2016,
postcards acquired by MoMA). Fits a human, small-data register; the hand-drawn mark still has to carry a comparison.

## Chart honesty

Alberto Cairo, *The Truthful Art* (New Riders, 2016) and *How Charts Lie* (W. W. Norton, 2019). Knaflic,
*Storytelling with Data* (Wiley, November 2015): context, declutter, focus attention, tell the story. Tufte, *The
Cognitive Style of PowerPoint* (2003, on NASA's Columbia slides): in technical settings, hand out a written report.
