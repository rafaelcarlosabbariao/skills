# Mythologies

## The claim

Everyday culture, wrestling matches, detergent ads, steak frites, the new Citroen DS, is a system of signs, and mass culture runs those signs through a second layer that converts contingent history into apparent nature. Barthes calls that second layer myth: a sign that has been emptied of its history and refilled with an ideological concept, then consumed as innocent fact. The mythologist's job is to catch the moment where "that's just how things are" is doing political work.

## Core claims

- Any cultural object can be read as a sign. The short essays (1954 to 1956, collected with the theoretical essay "Myth Today") are worked decodes of ads, food, film, sport, and press.
- Myth is a second-order semiological system: it does not sit beside language, it sits on top of it and parasitizes it.
- Myth transforms history into nature. Its function is not to hide meaning but to distort it, so the contingent reads as eternal and the political reads as common sense.
- Myth is depoliticized speech. It states things as fact and removes the memory of how they were made; the dominant class benefits precisely by never being named (ex-nomination).
- The reader of myth is the target: myth is built to be consumed whole, not decoded. Decoding is a deliberate act against the grain.
- Myth cannot be refuted by facts alone, because it concedes the facts and captures the frame. The counter-move Barthes proposes is to mythify the myth: make the second layer visible.

## Frameworks and models

**The two-tier sign (denotation, connotation, myth).** First order, from Saussure: signifier + signified = sign. Myth takes that completed first-order sign and uses it as the signifier of a second system. Barthes's terms: at the first level the sign is full of *meaning*; myth drains it into *form*, attaches a *concept*, and the pair yields a *signification*. Worked example from "Myth Today": a Paris Match cover, a Black soldier saluting the French flag. First order: a soldier salutes. Second order: French imperiality, the concept that empire is benign and all her sons serve without discrimination. The photo is true; the myth rides on its truth.

**The three reading positions.** (1) The producer reads the form as empty and fills it: this is how myths are made. (2) The mythologist reads form and concept together and sees the distortion: this is analysis. (3) The consumer reads the signification as a natural whole: this is how myth functions in the wild. Any audience splits across these positions, and the split is measurable.

**The rhetorical figures of myth.** Barthes names recurring moves: *inoculation* (confess a small evil to immunize the main one, the "Operation Margarine" essay), *privation of history* (the object appears from nowhere), *identification* (the other is reduced to a version of the self or exoticized), *tautology* ("it is what it is"), *neither-norism* (reject both sides to avoid the question), *quantification of quality* (effects counted, meaning skipped), *statement of fact* (proverb-voiced ideology). These are a working checklist, not a theory.

## Decision rules

- If a cultural claim presents as obvious, natural, or timeless, then treat it as a candidate myth: ask what history was removed and who benefits from the removal.
- If a brand or creator prominently admits a flaw, then check for inoculation before crediting transparency.
- If a position refuses paraphrase (tautology) or refuses both poles (neither-norism), then log the figure rather than arguing the content.
- If you want to date a myth, then look for the moment its vocabulary stopped needing justification: naturalization has a timestamp.
- If you need to counter a myth, then do not fact-check the first order; surface the second order, or build an artificial myth that exposes the form.
- Before naming any mechanism in a read, run the pre-pass: decode the sign, name the code, state the gap between what the sign promises and what the system delivers.

## Key terms

- **myth**: a second-order sign system that presents historical, motivated meaning as natural fact.
- **denotation**: the first-order, literal meaning of a sign.
- **connotation**: the second-order meaning a sign carries in a given culture.
- **form**: the first-order sign, drained of its history so myth can use it.
- **concept**: the ideological content myth attaches to the form.
- **signification**: the completed second-order sign, form plus concept, as consumed.
- **ex-nomination**: the dominant class's trick of never being named, so its interests read as universal.
- **inoculation**: admitting a contained evil to protect the institution from the principal one.
- **depoliticized speech**: myth's register: statements of fact with the politics removed.
