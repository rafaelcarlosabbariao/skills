# Course in General Linguistics

## The claim

Language is not a list of names for pre-existing things. It is a self-contained system of signs in which every unit gets its meaning from its differences from the other units, and the bond between form and concept is held by social convention alone. Because that bond is arbitrary, the apparatus generalizes: any convention-based sign system, from fashion to ritual, can be studied the same way, under a science Saussure names semiology. The book is posthumous, compiled by Bally and Sechehaye from students' notes on his Geneva lectures of 1907 to 1911.

## Core claims

- The sign is a two-sided unit: signifier (the form) plus signified (the concept). Neither exists independently.
- The bond between signifier and signified is arbitrary. Nothing in "dog" resembles a dog. Community convention is the only glue.
- Langue (the shared system of rules and distinctions) must be separated from parole (individual acts of speaking). Science works on langue; parole is the evidence trail.
- Meaning is differential. A term has value only through what it is not: "sheep" means what it means partly because English also has "mutton" and French does not.
- Synchronic analysis (the system at one moment) and diachronic analysis (change over time) are distinct operations and must not be mixed. Speakers live inside the synchronic state; the history is invisible to them.
- Because the sign is arbitrary, it is both mutable (nothing anchors it, so it drifts) and immutable in the short run (no individual can change it, because the community holds it).

## Frameworks and models

**The dyadic sign.** Sign = signifier + signified, linked by convention. Two qualifiers: arbitrariness (the link is unmotivated) and linearity (signifiers unfold in time). Saussure allows relative motivation: compounds like "nineteen" are partly analyzable, so systems sit on a spectrum from fully arbitrary to partly motivated. No numeric parameters; the model is structural.

**Langue vs parole.** Langue is the shared code; parole is any individual utterance or act. The code can only be observed through acts, but the code, not the acts, is the object of study. Practitioner translation: posts are parole; the audience's rule set is langue.

**Synchrony vs diachrony.** The synchronic axis is a snapshot of the whole system of values at time t; the diachronic axis tracks how single elements change across states. Saussure's chess analogy: the board position is fully readable without knowing the move history, and each move resets a new synchronic state.

**Value (valeur).** A unit's value is set by (a) what it can be exchanged for (its signified) and (b) what it can be compared with and differs from (neighboring terms). Language is a system of differences without positive terms. Change one term and the values of its neighbors move.

**Syntagmatic and associative axes.** Every sign sits in two relation sets: syntagmatic (what it combines with in sequence) and associative, later called paradigmatic (what could substitute for it in that slot). A cultural choice is legible only against the paradigm of options not chosen.

**Semiology.** The projected general science of signs within social life, of which linguistics is one branch. This is the license for reading fashion, food, and gesture as sign systems.

## Decision rules

- If a sign's meaning feels natural or inevitable, then treat that as the finding: name the convention and the community holding it.
- If you want to read what a choice signals, then reconstruct the paradigm first: what were the live alternatives, and what did picking this one exclude.
- If a term's meaning is shifting, then check its neighbors: value is relational, so a drift in one term implies repricing across the set.
- If you are explaining a current state, then read it synchronically first. Origin stories do not explain what the sign does today for the people using it.
- If you have a pile of individual posts, then do not report parole as insight. Infer the langue: the shared rules that make those posts sayable.
- If a sign matters to a community, then expect drift over time and stability against any single actor. Nobody can move it alone; everybody moves it together.

## Key terms

- **sign**: the union of a signifier and a signified; the basic unit of a code.
- **signifier**: the perceptible form, the sound-image.
- **signified**: the concept the form is bound to.
- **arbitrariness**: the bond between signifier and signified is conventional, not natural.
- **langue**: the shared system of rules and distinctions held by a community.
- **parole**: individual acts of speech or expression; the observable evidence of langue.
- **synchronic**: analysis of the system as it stands at one moment.
- **diachronic**: analysis of change in elements across time.
- **value (valeur)**: a unit's meaning as fixed by its differences from neighboring units.
- **semiology**: the general science of signs in social life; linguistics is one branch.
