# Gossip and Ostracism Promote Cooperation in Groups

## The claim

In public-goods experiments, letting players gossip between rounds raises
cooperation, and gossip plus the power to ostracize sustains it: reputational talk
identifies free riders, exclusion disciplines them, and the threat of both keeps
strangers cooperative. The paper (Psychological Science 25(3): 656-664) gives the
functional claims of the gossip literature an experimental footing: gossip is
measurably load-bearing, not merely social.

## Core claims

- Groups whose members can pass reputational notes about each other sustain higher
  contributions in public-goods play than groups that cannot.
- Gossip combined with ostracism outperforms either alone: talk identifies
  defectors, exclusion enforces the finding, and the pairing sustains cooperation
  across rounds.
- Ostracized defectors raise their contributions on re-entry: exclusion reforms
  rather than merely punishes, because re-admission is worth buying with
  cooperation.
- Much of the effect is anticipatory: knowing that gossip and exclusion are possible
  deters defection before either is used.
- Reputation systems built from peer talk are a group-level immune system against
  free riding.

## Frameworks and models

**The gossip-ostracism pairing.** Talk without teeth and teeth without talk both
underperform; the sensor (gossip) and the effector (exclusion) work as one
mechanism. Predicts that communities lose cooperation when either half is removed.

**Deterrence by reputation.** The possibility of being talked about and excluded
changes behavior before any talk occurs, so the mechanism's reach exceeds its
visible activity. A quiet channel is not an idle one.

**Reform through re-entry.** Exclusion with a path back converts defectors;
exclusion without one merely removes them. The design of re-admission decides which
system a community runs.

## Decision rules

- If a community of strangers sustains cooperation, look for working peer-evaluation
  channels before crediting shared values; the channel is the mechanism.
- If a platform's design suppresses person-to-person evaluation (feed-ification
  replacing group talk, DM silos), expect free riding and slop tolerance to rise
  even with unchanged rules.
- Read an ostracism event (a cancellation, a ban wave, a community expulsion) as the
  effector arm of a reputation system, and judge the system by whether re-entry
  exists, since that decides reform versus removal.
- Weight anticipatory effects: measuring only visible sanctions undercounts the
  mechanism.
- Carry the lab boundary honestly: point estimates from small-group public-goods
  play do not transfer to platform scale; carry the mechanism and its direction,
  and re-measure magnitudes in situ.

## Quantitative parameters

Real but bounded: laboratory public-goods design (groups of six, multi-round, gossip
and ostracism as crossed conditions) with cooperation highest under gossip plus
ostracism and ostracized players contributing more on return. Carry the design and
direction; the point estimates are lab-scale and deliberately not transplanted to
platform populations.
