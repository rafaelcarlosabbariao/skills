# Grooming, Gossip and the Evolution of Language

## The claim

Language evolved as a cheap form of social grooming: talk about other people is how
humans service more relationships than time allows, police free riders, and hold
groups together. Gossip is the original social-intelligence technology, not a
degraded use of language.

## Core claims

- Primate group size is capped by grooming time; language breaks the cap by letting
  one speaker service several listeners at once, so conversation is grooming at a
  distance.
- Neocortex size predicts a natural human group of roughly 150, the scale at which
  everyone can still hold a social model of everyone else.
- Roughly two-thirds of freely chosen conversation time is social topics: who did
  what with whom, and whether they should have. This is the load-bearing use of
  language.
- Gossip is the enforcement channel for reputation: it spreads information about
  defectors and free riders faster and more cheaply than direct observation could.
- Conversation groups self-limit at about four active participants, which bounds how
  fast social information moves through face-to-face networks.
- The social uses of language predate and underwrite the informational ones; a
  theory of media that starts from information transfer has the phylogeny backwards.

## Frameworks and models

**Vocal grooming (grooming at a distance).** Talk substitutes for one-to-one
grooming and multiplies the relationships a time budget can service. Predicts that
social channels are maintenance infrastructure first and content channels second.

**The social brain hypothesis.** Cognitive capacity for social modeling, not
habitat or diet, sets group size. The ~150 ceiling is where norms still self-enforce
through direct peer knowledge; beyond it, enforcement needs institutions. (The
Library previously carried this only as the Rule of 150 inside the Tipping Point
entry; this chapter is the primary source.)

**Gossip as reputation policing.** Third-party talk is the cheap sensor network that
catches free riders. A community's gossip volume about a member is enforcement
activity, not noise.

## Decision rules

- If a corpus is dominated by social-topic talk, read infrastructure at work, not
  triviality; the two-thirds baseline is the expected condition, and deviation from
  it is the finding.
- If a community's active core approaches 150, expect norm enforcement through peer
  knowledge to start failing and formal moderation to appear; read moderation
  debates at that scale as a predictable phase change.
- If gossip volume about one person or brand spikes, read reputation policing in
  progress and look for the alleged norm breach before judging the talk itself.
- If a platform design shrinks conversational group size or removes third-party
  talk, expect its norm enforcement to weaken even where stated values are
  unchanged.
- Do not carry the numeric parameters across cultures or media unchanged: the
  conversation-sampling numbers are 1990s British samples, and the record says so.

## Quantitative parameters

Carried with their limits: neocortex-ratio regression predicts mean human group size
~148 (wide confidence band, roughly 100 to 230); free conversation samples run about
two-thirds social topics; conversation groups hold at about four active speakers;
primate grooming budgets top out near a fifth of waking time. The conversation
numbers are 1990s British data and must be re-measured, not assumed, elsewhere.
