# What is Gossip About? An Alternative Hypothesis

## The claim

Against Gluckman: people gossip to advance their own interests. Gossip is best read
as individual information management, the control of what flows about oneself and
one's rivals, with any group-maintaining effects arriving as byproducts of
self-interested transactions. The paper (Man 2(2): 278-285) is the transactionalist
rejoinder that turned gossip studies into a live debate rather than a settled
functionalism.

## Core claims

- Gossip is goal-directed individual behavior: the gossiper campaigns for their own
  standing and against rivals, and the group-functional reading mistakes the
  byproduct for the purpose.
- Gossip is information management: control over the flow of information about
  oneself and others is a scarce resource that individuals compete to hold.
- The gossiper protects their own interests in the act of telling; who benefits from
  a telling is therefore the first analytic question, not the last.
- Gossip channels are transactional networks: information moves as exchange, with
  tellers earning credit, alliance, or protection from the telling.
- The Gluckman and Paine readings generate different predictions: group maintenance
  predicts gossip serving shared values; information management predicts gossip
  serving the teller, and cases must be allowed to decide.

## Frameworks and models

**Information management.** Individuals compete to control what circulates about
themselves and rivals. A gossip network is a market in accounts, and position in it
is power. The skilled gossiper manages their own file while editing everyone
else's.

**The transactional reading.** Each telling is an exchange with a payoff for the
teller: credit for good information, alliance with the hearer, damage to a rival.
Trace the payoff, not just the content, and the network's structure becomes legible.

**The Gluckman-Paine debate.** Two live hypotheses for any gossip event: norm
enforcement for the group, or a move in the teller's own campaign. They predict
different first-tellers (the invested member versus the advantaged rival) and
different beneficiaries (the value versus the teller).

## Decision rules

- Before reading gossip as group maintenance, ask who benefits from this telling; if
  the teller's standing improves, the self-interest reading has first claim.
- Map originators against relayers: the account's first-teller is a campaign datum,
  and a network where the same actors always originate is a managed flow, not a
  moral commons.
- Read a leak as a move in a standing competition, and locate the competitor it
  serves before treating it as transparency.
- When the group-function and self-interest readings both fit, prefer the one that
  predicts who spoke first and what it earned them.
- Treat professionalized tellers (influencers, insider accounts, industry
  newsletters) as information managers at scale, and read their output as portfolio
  management before reading it as reporting.
