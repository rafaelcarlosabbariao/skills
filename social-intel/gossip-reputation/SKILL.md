# Gossip and Reputation: The Original Social Intelligence

This shelf holds four founding works on gossip. Together they form one working theory of
what talk about people does: Dunbar supplies the capacity and the medium (why humans talk
about each other at all, and how much), Gluckman the group function (what the talk
maintains), Paine the individual transaction (what the teller gets), and Feinberg the
experimental proof (what the talk measurably produces). For anyone reading culture through
online talk, this is the mechanism layer under the claim that reading culture's talk is
reading culture.

Two later works sit beside the founding four. Hartung, Krohn and Pirschtat (2019) asked
gossipers for their reasons and found information first and malice last, which puts self-report
under layer 3. Newman et al. (2025), the Reuters Institute's roster of news creators, has no
chapter here yet.

## The unified mental model

Treat any gossip system as a four-layer stack. Each layer has an owner on this shelf,
and a full read touches all four.

**Layer 1: the capacity.** Dunbar: language evolved as cheap social grooming.
Conversation services more relationships than one-to-one grooming ever could, the
neocortex caps the fully-modeled group near 150, and roughly two-thirds of freely
chosen talk is social topics. Gossip is not a degraded use of the channel; it is the
channel's original cargo. Any corpus of human talk should be expected to run mostly
on who-did-what, and a corpus that does not is the anomaly to explain.

**Layer 2: the group function.** Gluckman: the right to gossip is a privilege of
membership, and the talk maintains the group's unity and values. Judging conduct
rehearses the norms; scandal fights status wars in moral vocabulary without open
conflict; and what the group cannot say officially pools in its informal talk. Who
may gossip about whom maps the group's real boundary more sharply than any stated
rule.

**Layer 3: the transaction.** Paine's rejoinder: people gossip to advance their own
interests, and gossip is information management, the control of what circulates about
oneself and one's rivals. Every telling has a teller and a payoff. The group-level
functions of layer 2 arrive, when they arrive, as byproducts of these self-interested
exchanges. The first analytic question about any account is who benefits from its
circulation.

**Layer 4: the measured output.** Feinberg, Willer and Schultz: in public-goods
experiments, groups that can pass reputational notes sustain higher cooperation, and
gossip paired with ostracism sustains it best. Exclusion reforms defectors when
re-entry exists, and much of the effect is anticipatory: the possibility of being
talked about disciplines behavior before any talk occurs. The functional claims of
layers 1 and 2 have a lab result under them.

## Through-lines

1. **Gossip is a sensor-effector pair.** Talk identifies (sensor), exclusion enforces
   (effector), and either half alone underperforms. A community's health check is
   whether both halves work: peer talk that names conduct, and consequences that
   attach to the naming.
2. **The boundary is in the talk, not the roster.** Dunbar's 150, Gluckman's
   gossip-rights, and Feinberg's exclusion all locate the group's edge in who talks
   about whom with what standing, never in formal membership.
3. **Every account has a teller.** Paine's discipline applies to all four works: the
   corpus records tellings, not events. Reading gossip means reading speakers,
   stakes, and first-tellers, and a topic count that ignores tellers misses the
   mechanism.
4. **Reputation requires a stake.** Dunbar's speakers stake their credibility,
   Feinberg's players stake their membership. Speech with nothing at stake (machine
   output, anonymous flooding) is outside the mechanism and cannot be disciplined by
   it, which is why communities react to it as pollution.
5. **Quiet channels still work.** Feinberg's deterrence and Gluckman's unsaid both
   mean the mechanism's reach exceeds its visible activity. Absence of scandal is
   not absence of enforcement; it may be enforcement working perfectly.

## Where the works disagree

**Gluckman versus Paine is the shelf's live axis.** Group maintenance against
individual campaign. They generate different predictions: Gluckman expects gossip to
serve shared values and expects the group to close ranks around the talk; Paine
expects gossip to serve the teller and expects first-tellers to profit. Carry both as
reading positions and let the case decide: check who spoke first and what the telling
earned them (Paine wins) against whether the talk rehearses norms nobody individually
profits from (Gluckman wins). Most real corpora contain both, and the mix is the
finding.

**Dunbar versus both.** Dunbar's evolutionary account explains why the channel
exists but is agnostic between the two above at the event level; do not cite
capacity claims to settle transactional questions.

**Hartung's scope.** The 2019 study asks gossipers for their reasons and finds information
first and malice last, which sits with Paine's information management and leaves Gluckman's
group-level claim untested. It is self-report from one German sample of 134: carry the order of
the motives and re-measure locally, never the means.

**Feinberg's scope.** The lab result is direction, not magnitude: groups of six
playing for points are not platforms of millions. Carry the mechanism and re-measure
locally, never transplant the point estimates.

## How to use this shelf with the instruments

- **The unsaid (cultural tension):** Gluckman's pooling claim is the method.
  Find where informal talk collects what cannot be said officially; the open secrets
  are value conflicts fought as scandal about persons.
- **Who's talking (audience mapping):** Paine's question is the method. Map tellers,
  stakes, originator-versus-relayer ratios; a community's real structure is its
  information-flow structure.
- **Word (trend velocity):** Dunbar's medium. Word of mouth is grooming at a
  distance, and its capacity limits (group size, time budgets) bound how fast talk
  can actually carry.
- **Audience simulation:** membership via gossip-rights, the Gluckman-Paine pair as agent reading
  positions, ~150 as the peer-enforcement ceiling parameter.
- **Forecast ensemble:** Feinberg grounds the verified-humanity call: reputation mechanisms
  need identifiable, excludable actors, so proof-of-personhood is the mechanism's
  infrastructure demand arriving.
