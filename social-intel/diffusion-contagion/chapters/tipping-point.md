# The Tipping Point

## The claim

Social phenomena spread like epidemics: contagious, sensitive to small causes, and prone to one dramatic threshold moment, the tipping point. Whether something tips depends on three levers: the messenger (Law of the Few), the message (Stickiness Factor), and the environment (Power of Context). Read with its asterisk: the rare-influencers half is contradicted by later cascade research (Watts and Dodds 2007), so treat the book as a lever inventory, not a targeting manual.

## Core claims

- Ideas, products, and behaviors diffuse under epidemic logic: contagious, nonlinear, abrupt once a threshold is crossed.
- A small fraction of people do a disproportionate share of transmission work: Connectors, Mavens, and Salesmen.
- Stickiness lives in the message; small structural edits to presentation, not budget or volume, make content memorable and actionable.
- Behavior is radically context sensitive: minor environmental features (Broken Windows, group size) can tip epidemics on or off.
- Canonical case: Hush Puppies, roughly 30,000 pairs a year in 1994 to 430,000 in 1995, then about four times that, seeded by East Village kids, not the brand.
- The asterisk: Watts's simulations show large cascades are usually triggered by ordinary people and depend on a critical mass of the easily influenced, not on the seed's connectivity.

## Frameworks and models

**Three Rules of Epidemics.** Any social epidemic can be diagnosed on three axes: who carries it (few), what it is (stickiness), where it lands (context). To tip something, you rarely need all three; you need the one lever that is currently binding.

**Law of the Few (Connector, Maven, Salesman).** Connectors: networks spanning many social worlds; the mechanic is weak ties and reach (Milgram's small-world chains, median about six links, funneled through a handful of people). Mavens: information specialists who accumulate market knowledge and share it to help, starting credible word of mouth. Salesmen: persuaders who close through emotional contagion. No formal parameterization; the implicit model is a heavy-tailed degree and activity distribution where the top of the tail does most transmission.

**Stickiness Factor.** Memorability is an engineering problem solved by iterating on structure, not intensity. Evidence: Levanthal's tetanus study, where adding a campus map and clinic times lifted vaccination from about 3 percent to 28 percent while stronger fear appeals did nothing; Sesame Street's Distracter tests. Mechanic: reduce the action gap, make the message practical and personal.

**Power of Context.** Two submodels. Broken Windows: visible small disorder signals permission and tips behavior epidemics (the NYC subway cleanup case). Rule of 150: Dunbar's number caps the group size at which norms self-enforce through peer knowledge; Gore Associates splits plants at 150. Mechanic: thresholds on environmental cues and group size, not persuasion.

**The Watts correction (carry it with the book).** In threshold-model simulations, cascade size is governed by the distribution of individual adoption thresholds and network connectivity, not by who starts the cascade. If a critical mass of low-threshold people is connected, almost any seed tips it; if not, no influencer can.

## Decision rules

- If a trend jumps subcultures fast and intact, look for connector bridges; if it deepens inside one scene with rich detail, look for mavens.
- If a message reaches people but does not convert, treat it as a stickiness failure: edit structure and lower the action cost before buying reach.
- If behavior shifted without a new message, audit context first: environment, defaults, group size.
- If someone proposes "find the influencers," apply the Watts test: model susceptible mass and thresholds first; seed identity is secondary.
- If adoption is creeping linearly, do not extrapolate linearly; check for threshold structure that could produce a jump either way.
- If a community crosses roughly 150 active members, expect norm enforcement to weaken and factions or moderation needs to appear.

## Key terms

- Tipping point: the threshold moment at which spread shifts from slow accumulation to rapid, self-sustaining growth.
- Connector: a person whose weak ties span many social worlds, giving ideas cross-scene reach.
- Maven: an information accumulator who shares knowledge to help others, seeding credible word of mouth.
- Salesman: a persuader who transmits adoption through charisma and emotional contagion.
- Stickiness Factor: the message property that makes it memorable and actionable, engineered through structural edits.
- Power of Context: the sensitivity of behavior to small features of the environment and group size.
- Broken Windows: visible minor disorder signals permission and tips larger behavior epidemics.
- Rule of 150: Dunbar's limit on group size within which norms self-enforce through direct peer knowledge.
- Influentials hypothesis: rare special people drive cascades; contradicted by Watts and Dodds's threshold simulations.
