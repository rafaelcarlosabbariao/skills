# The Strength of Weak Ties

## The claim

Novel information does not travel through your close friends, because your close friends all know each other and already know what you know. It travels through acquaintances, the weak ties that bridge otherwise separate clusters, so whatever diffuses through weak ties reaches more people and crosses greater social distance. The paper (American Journal of Sociology 78(6): 1360-1380) is the origin point of network sociology: it derives macro outcomes, diffusion, mobility, community cohesion, from the micro structure of dyadic ties.

## Core claims

- Tie strength is a combination of four things: time spent, emotional intensity, intimacy (mutual confiding), and reciprocal services. Strong ties breed transitivity: your close friends become friends with each other.
- The forbidden triad: if A is strongly tied to both B and C, a total absence of any tie between B and C is the least likely configuration. This one local rule generates the global result.
- It follows that no strong tie is a bridge. Any tie that is the only path between two clusters must be weak. Bridges are where all cross-cluster traffic flows.
- Strong-tie networks are redundant: information circulating in a clique gets heard twice and dies there. Weak ties are the shortcuts information actually travels.
- Empirical anchor: among Granovetter's professional, technical, and managerial job changers in Newton, Massachusetts who found the job through a personal contact, only 16.7 percent saw that contact often, 55.6 percent occasionally, 27.8 percent rarely. The decisive lead came from the network's edge, not its core.
- Removing weak ties fragments a network far more than removing strong ties. Communities that are all cliques and no bridges cannot mobilize: Boston's West End failed to organize against urban renewal for exactly this structural reason.

## Frameworks and models

**Tie strength.** Not a metaphor, a measurable quantity: strength = f(time, emotional intensity, intimacy, reciprocal services). In the job data it is operationalized as contact frequency: often is at least twice a week, occasionally is more than once a year, rarely is once a year or less.

**The forbidden triad and the bridge theorem.** From "A-B strong and A-C strong implies some B-C tie," Granovetter derives that all bridges are weak ties. A local bridge of degree n is a tie whose endpoints would otherwise be n steps apart; higher n means a more valuable shortcut. This is the structural ancestor of Watts-Strogatz rewiring: a few long-range weak links collapse path lengths in a clustered network.

**Diffusion through bridges.** Anything spreading person to person covers more social distance per hop when it crosses weak ties. The 1983 revisit adds the boundary condition: only bridging weak ties matter, a weak tie inside your own cluster buys you nothing. And Centola and Macy later add the counterweight: this physics holds for simple contagions (information, one exposure suffices), not for costly behavior, which needs wide bridges.

**Micro to macro.** Clique-only communities have intensity without connectivity; weak ties knit cliques into a whole capable of collective action. Macro cohesion is carried by the weakest micro links.

## Decision rules

- If you want to know where novel information enters a scene, watch the acquaintance links at cluster edges, not the dense core. The core hears everything second.
- If a term, sound, or aesthetic circulates only inside one tight cluster, log it as redundant chatter. If it surfaces in a structurally distant cluster, a bridge fired: expect broad spread and start the clock.
- If you are scoring a person's diffusion value, count bridging weak ties, not follower volume or strong-tie loyalty. One local bridge of high degree beats a thousand in-cluster fans.
- If an intense community fails to mobilize or a trend will not leave its scene, diagnose missing bridges between cliques before blaming the content.
- If seeding awareness, buy bridges. If seeding adoption of costly behavior, switch chapters: weak ties carry the news, not the commitment.

## Key terms

- Weak tie: an acquaintance link, low on time, intensity, intimacy, and reciprocity.
- Strong tie: a close link, high on all four components, and therefore transitive.
- Bridge: a tie that is the only path between two parts of a network; always weak.
- Local bridge (degree n): a tie whose removal would leave its endpoints n steps apart.
- Forbidden triad: two strong ties from one person with no tie between the other two; empirically rare, theoretically load-bearing.
- Transitivity: the tendency of a friend of a friend to become a friend.
- Redundancy: repeated exposure to the same information inside a clique; the cost of strong ties.
