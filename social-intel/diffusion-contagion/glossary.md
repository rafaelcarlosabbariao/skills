# Glossary: diffusion-contagion shelf

Merged terms from all eight chapters, alphabetical. Each entry names the chapter that defines it; terms defined in two chapters carry both slugs.

**Big-seed marketing** (influentials-watts-dodds): seeding many ordinary individuals rather than a few high-reach ones; the reliable default when cascade triggering is near-random.

**Bridge** (strength-of-weak-ties): a tie that is the only path between two parts of a network; always a weak tie, and where all cross-cluster traffic flows.

**Broken Windows** (tipping-point): visible minor disorder signals permission and tips larger behavior epidemics; generalizes to visible low-grade slop in a feed licensing more slop.

**Cascade window** (influentials-watts-dodds): the region of (connectivity, threshold) space where global cascades are possible at all; outside it, no seed works.

**Centrality** (connected): how embedded a node is in the network; governs how early it catches whatever flows.

**Change agent** (diffusion-of-innovations): an outsider who promotes adoption on behalf of an institution; succeeds through opinion leaders, not around them.

**Characteristic path length (L)** (small-world-networks): average shortest-path distance over all vertex pairs; near random-graph values in the small-world regime.

**Clustering coefficient (C)** (small-world-networks): average fraction of a vertex's neighbor pairs that are themselves connected; stays near lattice values while L collapses.

**Coefficient of imitation (q)** (bass-new-product-growth): strength of social influence; how much each prior adopter raises the adoption hazard for the rest. Typical fitted values for durables run 0.3 to 0.5.

**Coefficient of innovation (p)** (bass-new-product-growth): per-period adoption probability from external influence alone; the intercept of the hazard. Typical fitted values run 0.01 to 0.03.

**Compatibility** (diffusion-of-innovations): fit of an innovation with existing values, experiences, and needs; one of the five perceived attributes driving rate of adoption.

**Complex contagion** (complex-contagions, small-world-networks): a behavior whose adoption requires reinforcement from multiple sources, not one exposure; spreads poorly over long thin ties.

**Confounding (shared environment)** (connected): clustering caused by common exposure rather than ties; one of the three rival explanations for clustering, alongside contagion and homophily.

**Connector** (tipping-point): a person whose weak ties span many social worlds, giving ideas cross-scene reach.

**Critical infectiousness** (small-world-networks): the transmission rate at which a thing reaches a large share of the network; falls as rewiring probability p rises.

**Critical mass** (diffusion-of-innovations): the adoption level at which an interactive innovation becomes self-sustaining; below it, the same innovation can die.

**Critical mass of the easily influenced** (influentials-watts-dodds): the percolating cluster of low-threshold people that actually carries a cascade; the real engine behind large cascades.

**Diffusion** (diffusion-of-innovations): the process by which an innovation is communicated through certain channels over time among members of a social system.

**Emotional contagion** (connected): transmission of affect through mimicry and synchronized expression; also one of Centola and Macy's four mechanisms that make a contagion complex.

**First purchase** (bass-new-product-growth): the only event the Bass model counts; replacement and repeat buying are out of scope.

**Forbidden triad** (strength-of-weak-ties): two strong ties from one person with no tie between the other two; empirically rare, and the local rule from which the bridge theorem follows.

**Global cascade** (influentials-watts-dodds): an adoption event that reaches a finite fraction of the whole network from a single seed.

**Hazard rate** (bass-new-product-growth): probability of adopting now given you have not yet; Bass makes it linear in cumulative adoption, f(t)/(1-F(t)) = p + qF(t).

**Heterophily** (diffusion-of-innovations): difference between communicating pairs; new information usually enters a system across a heterophilous link.

**Homophily** (connected, diffusion-of-innovations): the tendency to bond with similar others; speeds diffusion between peers, and mimics contagion in cross-sectional data.

**Hyperdyadic spread** (connected): influence that travels beyond the directly connected pair, out to friends of friends of friends.

**Induction** (connected): true person-to-person transmission of a behavior or state; the only one of the three clustering explanations that is actual spread.

**Influentials hypothesis** (influentials-watts-dodds, tipping-point): the claim that a minority of special individuals drives most large-scale social change; contradicted by threshold-model simulations.

**Local bridge (degree n)** (strength-of-weak-ties): a tie whose removal would leave its endpoints n steps apart; higher n means a more valuable shortcut.

**Market potential (m)** (bass-new-product-growth): the finite pool of eventual adopters; everything saturates against it, so post-peak decline is arithmetic, not failure.

**Maven** (tipping-point): an information accumulator who shares knowledge to help others, seeding credible word of mouth.

**Observability** (diffusion-of-innovations): how visible the results of adoption are to others; one of the five perceived attributes.

**Opinion leader** (diffusion-of-innovations): a member whose informal influence moves others' adoption; concentrated among early adopters.

**Peak time t*** (bass-new-product-growth): ln(q/p) / (p + q); when new adoptions top out, at penetration F* = 1/2 - p/(2q), always just under half of potential.

**Power of Context** (tipping-point): the sensitivity of behavior to small features of the environment and group size.

**Redundancy** (strength-of-weak-ties): repeated exposure to the same information inside a clique; the cost of strong ties, and the fuel of complex contagion.

**Reinvention** (diffusion-of-innovations): change in the innovation by adopters during implementation; usually speeds adoption, and shifts meaning down the curve.

**Relative advantage** (diffusion-of-innovations): perceived improvement over the thing the innovation replaces.

**Rewiring probability (p)** (small-world-networks, complex-contagions): per-edge chance of random reattachment; the one knob between lattice and random graph. Distinct from Bass's p.

**Rule of 150** (tipping-point): Dunbar's limit on group size within which norms self-enforce through direct peer knowledge.

**S-curve** (bass-new-product-growth): the cumulative adoption shape produced whenever q > p; Rogers's take-off sits at roughly 10 to 20 percent adoption.

**Salesman** (tipping-point): a persuader who transmits adoption through charisma and emotional contagion.

**Shortcut** (small-world-networks): a rewired long-range edge that contracts distance between two whole neighborhoods; one shortcut beats one edge.

**Simple contagion** (complex-contagions, small-world-networks): spread where a single contact can transmit, as with information or disease; benefits directly from short paths.

**Small-world regime** (small-world-networks): the band of rewiring probability where path length is near random while clustering is still near lattice.

**Stickiness Factor** (tipping-point): the message property that makes it memorable and actionable; engineered through structural edits, not intensity.

**Strategic complementarity** (complex-contagions): payoff to adopting rises with the number of prior adopters; one of the four mechanisms making a contagion complex.

**Strong tie** (strength-of-weak-ties): a close link, high on time, emotional intensity, intimacy, and reciprocal services; therefore transitive, therefore never a bridge.

**Three degrees of influence** (connected): empirical rule that social influence decays to noise beyond friends of friends of friends; the default organic reach horizon.

**Threshold (T, phi)** (complex-contagions, influentials-watts-dodds): the number (T) or fraction (phi) of activated neighbors a node needs before it activates. T = 1 defines a simple contagion; T >= 2 defines a complex one.

**Tie range** (complex-contagions): the topological distance a tie spans, distinct from its relational strength; the variable doing causal work in the long-ties result.

**Tie strength** (strength-of-weak-ties): a combination of time spent, emotional intensity, intimacy, and reciprocal services; measurable, not a metaphor.

**Tipping point** (tipping-point): the threshold moment at which spread shifts from slow accumulation to rapid, self-sustaining growth.

**Transitivity** (strength-of-weak-ties, connected): the tendency for two of one person's contacts to be connected to each other; strong ties breed it, and it is what makes cliques redundant.

**Trialability** (diffusion-of-innovations): how easily the innovation can be tested at small scale; one of the five perceived attributes.

**Two-step flow** (influentials-watts-dodds): Katz and Lazarsfeld's model where media influence reaches the public via opinion leaders; survives casual observation, fails when formalized.

**Vulnerable node** (influentials-watts-dodds): one tippable by a single active neighbor, requiring degree k <= 1/phi; global cascades need vulnerable nodes to form a percolating cluster.

**Weak tie** (strength-of-weak-ties): an acquaintance link, low on all four strength components; the only kind of tie that can be a bridge.

**Wide bridge** (complex-contagions): multiple overlapping ties between two clusters, wide enough to deliver T exposures across the gap; the operational replacement for influencer reach.
