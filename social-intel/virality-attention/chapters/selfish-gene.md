# The Selfish Gene

## The claim

Evolution is best read from the replicator's point of view, not the organism's. Genes are replicators, bodies are survival machines built to carry them, and what persists is whatever copies itself well, whether or not it benefits the carrier. Chapter 11 extends the logic to culture: a meme copies brain to brain by imitation and spreads on its own replication advantage, not on its truth or its use to its host.

## Core claims

- The unit of selection is the replicator, not the group or the individual. Ask what is being copied, then what favors the copy.
- Replicator success reduces to longevity, fecundity, and copying-fidelity. Any substrate supporting all three gets an evolutionary process for free.
- Organisms are vehicles. In culture the vehicles are people, feeds, creators, and institutions, and their interests diverge from the replicator's.
- Meme fitness is independent of truth and of host benefit. A meme can spread while damaging its carriers, the way a virus parasitizes a host cell.
- Memes travel in co-adapted complexes, so the bundle is the real field unit.
- Stable outcomes are frequency-dependent. Competing strategies end at a ratio, not a winner.

## Frameworks and models

**Replicator triad.** Longevity (how long a copy survives), fecundity (copies per unit time), copying-fidelity (how faithfully it reproduces). Score any spreading artifact on all three. High fecundity plus low fidelity is churn. High fidelity plus low fecundity is a slow burn. Both high is regime change.

**Replicator and vehicle.** The replicator is copied; the vehicle carries it. Confusing them means reading a trend as a statement about a creator who is only the current vehicle.

**Meme (chapter 11).** From the Greek mimeme, shortened to rhyme with gene. Memes propagate through a meme pool by leaping brain to brain via imitation, competing for one scarce resource: attention and memory. Dawkins' examples are tunes, catch-phrases, clothes fashions, ways of making pots. Survival value comes from psychological appeal and imitability, not correctness.

**Co-adapted meme complexes.** Memes that raise each other's survival odds once co-resident in a host. Dawkins' case is the god meme bundled with faith, hell, and taboo. Practitioner version: positions arrive prepackaged.

**Evolutionarily stable strategy (ESS), after Maynard Smith.** Dawkins' hawk-dove payoffs: win +50, serious injury -100, time wasted in a long staring match -10. Hawk vs hawk averages -25, hawk vs dove +50, dove vs dove +15, dove vs hawk 0. Equal payoffs give a stable mix of 7/12 hawks to 5/12 doves, averaging 6.25 per contest. An all-dove population averages 15, better for everyone and still not stable, because one hawk invades it. Frequency dependence, not optimality, sets the outcome.

**Kin selection and reciprocity.** Altruism pays when relatedness times benefit exceeds cost (r = 1/2 for a parent or sibling, 1/8 for a first cousin). Chapter 12, added 1989, brings in tit for tat: nice, retaliatory, forgiving, clear.

## Decision rules

- If something is spreading, score longevity, fecundity, and fidelity separately. The three predict different curve shapes.
- If a format degrades on each retelling, expect a short arc. If it survives retelling intact (a template, a sound, a fixed phrasing), expect persistence.
- If a meme spreads while visibly costing its carriers time, money, or standing, do not infer that it serves them. Ask what the meme gains.
- If a belief arrives bundled with three others, track the complex. Refuting one item leaves it intact.
- If two behaviors coexist at a steady ratio, stop hunting for a winner. The ratio is the finding.
- If truth is your explanation for spread, discard it. Truth is not a fitness term, imitability is.
- If ranking changes, that is selection pressure changing, not taste.

## Key terms

- **Replicator**: anything that makes copies of itself; the unit selection acts on.
- **Vehicle (survival machine)**: the carrier a replicator occupies; a body, a person, a feed, a brand.
- **Meme**: a unit of cultural transmission propagating by imitation from brain to brain.
- **Meme pool**: the memes competing for attention and memory in a culture.
- **Copying-fidelity**: how faithfully a replicator reproduces per transmission; best predictor of persistence.
- **Fecundity**: copies per unit time; predicts amplitude, not duration.
- **Co-adapted meme complex**: a mutually reinforcing bundle of memes, more stable together than apart.
- **ESS**: a strategy or mix that, once common, cannot be invaded.
