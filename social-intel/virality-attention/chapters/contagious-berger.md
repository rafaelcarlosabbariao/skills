# Contagious: Why Things Catch On

## The claim

Virality is a property of the message, not the messenger. Six psychological drivers (STEPPS) determine whether people talk about and share a thing, and any product, idea, or behavior can be engineered along them. Word of mouth, which is mostly offline, is the primary engine of adoption, so the practitioner's job is to design for transmission rather than hunt for influencers.

## Core claims

- Contagious content is made, not born. Against the Gladwell-style Law of the Few: message design beats messenger selection.
- Word of mouth is the dominant adoption channel and mostly invisible to platforms: roughly 7 percent of it happens online (Keller Fay), and it sits behind an estimated 20 to 50 percent of purchase decisions.
- Sharing is self-interested. People pass on what makes them look good (social currency) and what helps others (practical value).
- Talk frequency is governed by cues, not quality. Interesting things earn immediate word of mouth; only environmentally triggered things earn ongoing word of mouth.
- Arousal, not valence, drives transmission. Awe, anger, anxiety, excitement, and humor all raise sharing; sadness and contentment suppress it (Berger and Milkman, Journal of Marketing Research, 2012).
- Observability compounds spread: public behaviors get imitated, private ones stall. Built to show, built to grow.
- Stories are carriers, but only "valuable virality," where the idea is integral to the narrative and survives retelling, converts attention into adoption.

## Frameworks and models

**STEPPS**, six independent levers. A thing does not need all six; each present lever raises transmission probability.

- **Social Currency**: sharing as status signaling. Mechanics: inner remarkability, game mechanics with visible status tiers, scarcity and exclusivity (the Please Don't Tell speakeasy hidden inside a hot dog shop).
- **Triggers**: top of mind means tip of tongue. Link the idea to a frequent cue in the audience's daily habitat (Kit Kat paired with coffee breaks; Rebecca Black's "Friday" spiking every Friday). Trigger frequency and habitat fit predict ongoing word of mouth better than cleverness does.
- **Emotion**: when we care, we share, but only if the emotion activates. From the New York Times most-emailed corpus of roughly 7,000 articles: awe raised the odds of making the list by about 30 percent; sadness lowered them by about 16 percent. Anger and anxiety, though negative, increase sharing because they are high arousal.
- **Public**: make the private observable. Movember mustaches, Livestrong bands, behavioral residue that keeps signaling after the act. Caution: publicizing a bad behavior normalizes it, which is why some anti-drug campaigns backfired.
- **Practical Value**: news you can use, packaged for forwarding. The Rule of 100: below 100 dollars, frame discounts as percentages; above 100 dollars, frame them as absolute amounts. Built on prospect theory reference points and diminishing sensitivity.
- **Stories**: information travels inside narrative like a Trojan horse. The test is whether the core idea survives casual retelling. Panda cheese passes; Evian's roller babies got views without moving water.

Quantitative anchors: immediate versus ongoing word of mouth as separate outcome variables; arousal, not positivity, as the sharing predictor; the 7 percent online share of word of mouth as a standing correction to platform-only measurement.

## Decision rules

- If a trend is spiking, identify which STEPPS lever carries it before forecasting. Arousal-driven spikes decay fast; trigger-driven ones persist.
- If content is widely seen but rarely shared, audit social currency: does passing it on raise or lower the sharer's status.
- If you need to forecast persistence, count trigger frequency in the audience's daily habitat, not launch buzz.
- If a story is huge but the subject is detachable from it, discount the adoption effect. Virality without value is noise.
- If a behavior lives in a private domain, expect slower spread than in observable domains at equal interest, and watch for public proxies to emerge before calling a takeoff.
- If sharing collapses on a topic, check whether coverage went low arousal (sad, resigned) before concluding interest died.

## Key terms

- **STEPPS**: Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories; the six drivers of word of mouth.
- **Social currency**: the status value of sharing something; people share what makes them look smart, connected, or in the know.
- **Trigger**: an environmental cue that brings an idea back to mind and back into conversation.
- **Immediate vs ongoing word of mouth**: talk right after exposure versus talk sustained over weeks; interest drives the first, triggers drive the second.
- **High-arousal emotion**: physiologically activating states (awe, anger, anxiety, excitement, humor) that increase sharing regardless of valence.
- **Behavioral residue**: physical traces a behavior leaves behind that keep advertising it after the act.
- **Rule of 100**: frame discounts as percentages under 100 dollars and as absolute amounts above it.
- **Valuable virality**: spread where the brand or idea is integral to the story and survives retelling; the opposite is attention that transmits nothing.
