# Glossary: virality and attention

Merged across all five chapters on this shelf. Slug in parentheses is the chapter to open for the full treatment. Where two works use the same word differently, both entries appear.

**Arousal** (what-makes-content-viral): physiological activation, the mobilized state some emotions induce. The paper's core predictor of sharing, and a stronger lever than valence.

**Arousal-valence grid** (what-makes-content-viral): two orthogonal axes for classifying any emotional payload. Valence runs positive to negative, arousal runs activating to deactivating. High-arousal positive is awe, amusement, excitement; high-arousal negative is anger and anxiety; low-arousal positive is contentment; low-arousal negative is sadness. Predict transmission from the arousal row first, then adjust for valence.

**Awe** (what-makes-content-viral; contagious-berger): high-arousal response to vastness that demands mental accommodation, a forced frame update. Strongly pro-viral, and it works even on long or cognitively demanding material. In the NYT field model, a one standard deviation increase in rated awe raised the odds of making the most-emailed list by about 30 percent.

**Behavioral residue** (contagious-berger): physical traces a behavior leaves behind that keep advertising it after the act is over. Livestrong bands, Movember mustaches.

**Bhattacharyya distance** (true-false-news): one of three distance measures used to score how novel a tweet's topic mix was against the sharer's prior 60 days of exposure.

**Bot null result** (true-false-news): removing automated accounts from the dataset left the true-false gap intact. Bots accelerated true and false news at roughly the same rate, so humans produced the differential.

**Cascade depth** (true-false-news): number of retweet hops from the origin tweet. Truth took about twenty times as long as falsehood to reach depth 10.

**Cascade size** (true-false-news): the count of unique users in a rumor cascade. One of four cascade measurements, never to be reported alone.

**Co-adapted meme complex** (selfish-gene): a mutually reinforcing bundle of memes that raise each other's survival odds once co-resident in a host, and that is more stable together than apart. Dawkins' case is the god meme bundled with faith, hell, and taboo. Practitioner version: positions arrive prepackaged, and refuting one item leaves the complex intact.

**Community concentration** (predicting-successful-memes): the degree to which a meme's early adopters cluster inside few network communities. The strongest predictor of final popularity in the paper.

**Complex contagion** (predicting-successful-memes): spread requiring reinforcement from multiple contacts. Dense within-community ties supply that reinforcement, so complex-contagion memes get trapped inside the community that incubated them. Identity-heavy or costly behaviors usually run in this regime.

**Copying-fidelity** (selfish-gene): how faithfully a replicator reproduces per transmission. The best single predictor of persistence. A format that degrades on each retelling has a short arc; one that survives retelling intact persists.

**Deactivating emotion** (what-makes-content-viral): an emotion (sadness, contentment) that lowers action readiness and suppresses sharing.

**Emotional signature** (true-false-news): the reply-emotion profile of a claim, scored against the NRC emotion lexicon. Surprise plus disgust for false claims; sadness, anticipation, joy, and trust for true ones.

**Evolutionarily stable strategy (ESS)** (selfish-gene): after Maynard Smith, a strategy or mix of strategies that, once common, cannot be invaded by an alternative. The outcome is set by frequency dependence, not by optimality.

**Fecundity** (selfish-gene): copies produced per unit time. Predicts amplitude, not duration.

**Frequency dependence** (selfish-gene): the property that a strategy's payoff depends on how common it currently is, which is why competing strategies settle at a ratio rather than at a winner. In the hawk-dove payoffs (win +50, serious injury -100, long staring match -10), equal payoffs give a stable mix of 7/12 hawks to 5/12 doves.

**Ground truth by fact-checker consensus** (true-false-news): the design pattern of defining veracity exogenously before measuring diffusion, never the reverse. Six independent fact-checking organizations agreed 95 to 98 percent of the time, with a hand-coded robustness sample.

**Hawk-dove payoffs** (selfish-gene): Dawkins' worked ESS example. Hawk against hawk averages -25, hawk against dove +50, dove against dove +15, dove against hawk 0. The stable mix averages 6.25 per contest, while an all-dove population would average 15 and still be unstable, because one hawk invades it.

**High-arousal emotion** (contagious-berger): physiologically activating states (awe, anger, anxiety, excitement, humor) that increase sharing regardless of valence.

**Immediate versus ongoing word of mouth** (contagious-berger): talk right after exposure versus talk sustained over weeks. Interest drives the first, triggers drive the second. They are separate outcome variables and must not be swapped mid-argument.

**Incidental arousal** (what-makes-content-viral): activation from an unrelated source, such as exercise, that still boosts sharing of unrelated content. From companion work (Berger 2011), and evidence that arousal is the mechanism rather than a correlate.

**Infected communities** (predicting-successful-memes): the count of distinct network communities containing at least one early adopter of a meme.

**Infomap** (predicting-successful-memes): the community-detection algorithm the authors run on the follower graph. The finding is robust to the choice of detection method.

**Intra-community interaction fraction** (predicting-successful-memes): the share of early adoption edges (retweets, mentions, follows) that stay inside a single community rather than bridging out. Third of the three concentration measures.

**Kin selection** (selfish-gene): altruism pays when relatedness times benefit exceeds cost, with r = 1/2 for a parent or sibling and 1/8 for a first cousin.

**KL divergence** (true-false-news): Kullback-Leibler divergence, one of three distances used in the novelty scoring against a user's 60-day exposure baseline.

**Latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA)** (true-false-news): the topic model used to represent each tweet and each user's recent exposure so that novelty could be measured as a distance between distributions.

**Law of the Few** (contagious-berger): the Gladwell-style position that a handful of special messengers carry adoption. Berger argues against it: message design beats messenger selection.

**Limited attention economy** (predicting-successful-memes; true-false-news): the regime where ideas exceed available cognitive bandwidth, so attention scarcity rather than idea supply determines survival. Memes compete ecologically inside it, and novelty is the currency that wins the competition.

**Longevity** (selfish-gene): how long a single copy survives. First term of the replicator triad.

**Maximum breadth** (true-false-news): the largest number of users reached at any single depth of a cascade. Separates a wide shallow reach from a deep chain.

**Meme** (selfish-gene): a unit of cultural transmission that propagates by imitation from brain to brain. From the Greek mimeme, shortened to rhyme with gene. Dawkins' examples are tunes, catch-phrases, clothes fashions, ways of making pots. Survival value comes from psychological appeal and imitability, not correctness.

**Meme, operationalized** (predicting-successful-memes): in the paper, a new Twitter hashtag on a reciprocal-follow network of hundreds of thousands of users. The measurable stand-in for a unit of transmissible culture.

**Meme pool** (selfish-gene): the population of memes competing for the scarce resources of attention and memory in a culture.

**Most-emailed list** (what-makes-content-viral): the New York Times sharing chart used as the field outcome. A proxy for transmission and a biased sample of culture, because it over-represents arousing content.

**Novelty (information uniqueness)** (true-false-news): the distance between a tweet's topic mix and the sharer's prior 60-day exposure. False rumors scored more novel on all three distance measures, and novelty predicted sharing. Novelty earns attention in a limited-attention economy and confers insider status on the sharer.

**NRC emotion lexicon** (true-false-news): the lexicon used to score the emotional content of replies, producing the emotional signature of true versus false cascades.

**Odds ratio 1.7** (true-false-news): falsehood's 70 percent retweet advantage over truth, from a logistic regression holding follower count, followees, account age, activity, and verification fixed. The single most portable parameter on this shelf: a fixed multiplier on transmission probability for novel-surprising claims.

**Practical utility** (what-makes-content-viral; contagious-berger): usable value in content that drives sharing through the exchange channel, independent of emotion. In Berger's STEPPS it is the Practical Value lever, packaged for forwarding; the Rule of 100 is its worked example.

**Public** (contagious-berger): the STEPPS lever for observability. Built to show, built to grow. Public behaviors get imitated and private ones stall. Caution: publicizing a bad behavior normalizes it, which is why some anti-drug campaigns backfired.

**Random forests** (predicting-successful-memes): the classifier used to predict whether a hashtag's final popularity lands in a top percentile bracket, fed by features from only the first roughly 50 tweets.

**Replicator** (selfish-gene): anything that makes copies of itself. The unit selection acts on, and the thing you must name before measuring a trend.

**Replicator triad** (selfish-gene): longevity, fecundity, and copying-fidelity, scored separately because each predicts a different curve shape. High fecundity with low fidelity is churn. High fidelity with low fecundity is a slow burn. Both high is regime change.

**Rule of 100** (contagious-berger): frame discounts as percentages below 100 dollars and as absolute amounts above it. Built on prospect theory reference points and diminishing sensitivity.

**Rumor cascade** (true-false-news): one origin assertion plus its unbroken retweet chain. The unit of diffusion analysis in the paper, and the correct unit for any claim about whether something spread.

**Simple contagion** (predicting-successful-memes): spread requiring only a single exposure. It leaks across the weak ties between communities the way a disease does, which is why viral memes cross community boundaries early.

**Social currency** (contagious-berger): the status value of sharing something. People pass on what makes them look smart, connected, or in the know. Mechanics include inner remarkability, game mechanics with visible status tiers, and scarcity or exclusivity.

**Social transmission** (what-makes-content-viral): person-to-person passing of content. The paper's dependent behavior.

**STEPPS** (contagious-berger): Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories. Six independent levers of word of mouth. A thing does not need all six; each present lever raises transmission probability.

**Stories** (contagious-berger): the STEPPS lever for narrative carriage. Information travels inside narrative like a Trojan horse, but the test is whether the core idea survives casual retelling. Panda cheese passes; Evian's roller babies got views without moving water.

**Structural trapping** (predicting-successful-memes): the confinement of a reinforcement-dependent meme inside the dense community that incubated it.

**Structural virality** (true-false-news): mean shortest-path distance between all node pairs in a cascade (Goel, Anderson, Hofman and Watts). Low means one broadcast hub; high means true peer-to-peer diffusion. Separates being amplified from actually spreading.

**Tit for tat** (selfish-gene): the reciprocity strategy from chapter 12, added in 1989. Nice, retaliatory, forgiving, clear.

**Trigger** (contagious-berger): an environmental cue that brings an idea back to mind and back into conversation. Top of mind means tip of tongue. Kit Kat paired with coffee breaks; Rebecca Black's "Friday" spiking every Friday. Trigger frequency and habitat fit predict ongoing word of mouth better than cleverness does.

**Two-channel transmission** (what-makes-content-viral): channel one is emotional broadcasting, where arousal discharges into sharing; channel two is social exchange, where useful or interesting content is shared to help, signal, or bond. Score both separately on any spreading artifact.

**Usage entropy** (predicting-successful-memes): Shannon entropy of a meme's early adoption across communities, H = -sum over communities c of p(c) log p(c), where p(c) is the fraction of early tweets or adopters in community c. Low entropy means concentrated, high entropy means dispersed and signals viral potential. If you can afford only one metric, compute this one.

**Valence** (what-makes-content-viral): the positive or negative feel of content. A weaker and secondary predictor of sharing than arousal, though positive beats negative on average once arousal is controlled.

**Valuable virality** (contagious-berger): spread where the brand or idea is integral to the story and survives retelling. The opposite is attention that transmits nothing.

**Vehicle (survival machine)** (selfish-gene): the carrier a replicator occupies. A body, a person, a feed, a brand. Vehicle interests and replicator interests diverge, so a meme can spread while damaging its carriers.

**Word of mouth, offline share** (contagious-berger): roughly 7 percent of word of mouth happens online (Keller Fay), and word of mouth sits behind an estimated 20 to 50 percent of purchase decisions. A standing correction to platform-only measurement.
