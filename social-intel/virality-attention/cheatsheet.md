# Cheatsheet: virality and attention

Fast lookups. Every row traces to a chapter on this shelf. Nothing here is a substitute for reading the chapter when the call is consequential.

---

## 1. Situation to model

| Situation | Which model applies | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Something is rising and you must call wave or pocket | Community concentration (predicting-successful-memes) | Usage entropy of the first ~50 adopters across communities. Ignore the total. |
| A spike needs interpreting before it is written up | Arousal-valence grid (what-makes-content-viral) | Which activating emotion is fueling it. Anger, anxiety, awe, or amusement. |
| A claim is moving unusually fast and deep | Cascade anatomy plus novelty (true-false-news) | Velocity-depth anomaly, then reply-emotion signature. Verify before quoting. |
| You need half-life, not amplitude | Replicator triad (selfish-gene) plus trigger habitat (contagious-berger) | Copying-fidelity under casual retelling, and how many daily cues the unit is anchored to. |
| Something is widely seen but rarely shared | Social currency (contagious-berger) | Does passing it on raise or lower the sharer's status. |
| A domain has gone quiet | Deactivating emotion (what-makes-content-viral) | Whether coverage went sad-coded or low arousal before concluding interest died. |
| A niche behavior is being extrapolated to the mainstream | Simple versus complex contagion (predicting-successful-memes) | Whether adoption needs social cover. If yes, expect structural trapping. |
| Views and shares disagree | Two-channel transmission (what-makes-content-viral) plus replicator and vehicle (selfish-gene) | Split the layers and report the divergence as the finding. |
| A belief will not die despite being debunked | Co-adapted meme complex (selfish-gene) | Which other items arrive bundled with it. Refuting one leaves the complex intact. |
| Two positions have coexisted at a stable ratio | ESS and frequency dependence (selfish-gene) | Plot the ratio, not the levels. If stable, the ratio is the finding. |
| Deciding between candidate framings for a piece | Arousal class (what-makes-content-viral) | Bet the anger or anxiety framing over the sad framing. Score awe potential for long material. |
| Content is useful but emotionally flat | Exchange channel (what-makes-content-viral; contagious-berger) | Practical utility and surprise before writing it off. |
| Someone blames bots for a spread differential | Bot null result (true-false-news) | Demand the human-only rerun. Bots explain volume, not the gap. |
| A most-shared list is being read as public opinion | Sampling bias (what-makes-content-viral) | Reclassify the top items by arousal. Share counts measure activation. |
| A trend is being described entirely through one creator | Replicator and vehicle (selfish-gene) | Name the unit that copies. If you cannot, it is not a trend yet. |
| A story is huge but the subject is detachable from it | Valuable virality (contagious-berger) | Whether the core idea survives casual retelling. If not, discount the adoption effect. |
| You are ranking several candidate trends | Community dispersion over early counts (predicting-successful-memes) | Dispersion. Never headline on early volume. |
| A campaign or product brief asks how to make it spread | STEPPS (contagious-berger) | Which levers are present, and which one will carry it. |

## 2. Curve shape from the replicator triad

| Fecundity | Fidelity | Expected shape | Call it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | Low | Sharp spike, fast decay | Churn. Base rate, not a shift. |
| Low | High | Slow accumulation, long tail | Slow burn. Under-reported early. |
| High | High | Step function, sustained | Regime change. |
| Low | Low | Noise | Not a trend. |

Source: selfish-gene. Longevity is the third term and modifies all four rows by how long an individual copy stays intelligible.

## 3. Trend domains

| Domain | Dominant mechanism on this shelf | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Language | Triggers, and the highest-fidelity domain. Also the literal unit of predicting-successful-memes (new hashtags). | Trigger anchoring to a daily cue, and whether the coinage survives misquotation. Orphaned coinages die. |
| Fashion | Public-dominant, so observable and fast to imitate. High fecundity, low fidelity, so churn is the base rate. | Whether fidelity has risen (a named silhouette) before calling a shift. |
| Body | Public-dominant, same churn base rate as fashion. Also share-driven and arousal-weighted. | Whether fidelity has risen (a reproducible protocol), and which arousal emotion is carrying the discourse. |
| Music | Social currency spikes, taste signaling in public. Short-form gives near-perfect fidelity to a sound. | Expect sharp step functions. Check community dispersion of early adopters. |
| Food | Social currency spikes. Short-form gives near-perfect fidelity to a recipe. | Same as music: step functions, and dispersion over counts. |
| Tech | Share-driven signal, arousal-weighted. | The fueling emotion before treating loudness as prevalence. |
| Relationships | Share-driven signal, arousal-weighted. | The fueling emotion, and whether the behavior is private (slower spread, watch for public proxies). |
| Work | No work-specific hook in these chapters. Apply the general spine. | Wave-or-pocket entropy check, then the arousal pre-pass. |

The wave-or-pocket structural test and the arousal correction layer both apply to all 8 domains. The rows above only say which mechanism tends to dominate.

## 4. Pressure systems

| System | Mechanism on this shelf | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| slop (human vs AI slop) | Generative systems push fecundity and fidelity toward the limit while decoupling the unit from host benefit. Distribution still requires human appetite for novelty. Slop floods the competition pool and raises the kill rate for everything else. | The share layer, not the view layer. Predicted equilibrium is slop winning views while human work wins transmission. Track novelty saturation per domain. |
| taste (taste vs the algorithm) | A social currency story: feeds optimize impressions, people share what signals taste. The recommender is the selection environment and ranking is the fitness function. The falsehood advantage was measured largely before heavy algorithmic ranking, so it is human preference that the algorithm amplifies. | Whether a ranking change (selection pressure) is being misread as a taste change. |
| values (the values split) | An anger and anxiety machine, and a co-adapted meme complex. Political content carries the largest falsehood premium. | Read volume as arousal, not consensus. Track bundles rather than items. Highest verification bar on sourced claims. |
| gender (the gender war) | Same as values: arousal-fueled, complex-shaped, largest falsehood premium. | Expect share of voice to overstate real-world division. Apply the two-step velocity gate to any claim. |
| lonely (the loneliness economy) | No direct hook in these five chapters. Apply the general rule. | Sadness is deactivating and suppresses sharing, so quiet around grief-heavy topics is not evidence of absent feeling. Check whether coverage went sad-coded before reading a collapse. |

## 6. Red flags in a draft read

- A raw count is doing the work of an argument. Replace with entropy, depth, breadth, or structural virality.
- The word "engagement" appears instead of a named emotion.
- Early volume is being used to forecast a ceiling. The paper says it is a weak predictor.
- Quality or truth is offered as the explanation for spread. Neither is a fitness term.
- A share spike is being read as a change in belief. It is activation.
- A creator is being analyzed where a replicating unit should be.
- One debunk is being forecast to collapse a bundled position.
- A scene is being extrapolated to the mainstream without a complex-contagion check.
- A cascade is being discounted because its spreaders are small and unverified. That is the normal profile of a false cascade.
- Immediate and ongoing word of mouth are being swapped mid-argument, or a top-percentile popularity outcome is being compared to a cascade-geometry outcome.

## 7. Which outcome variable are you actually predicting

| Outcome variable | Source | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate word of mouth | contagious-berger | You care about launch-week talk. Driven by interest. |
| Ongoing word of mouth | contagious-berger | You care about persistence. Driven by triggers. |
| Final popularity in a top percentile | predicting-successful-memes | You are ranking candidates and want a ceiling call from early structure. |
| Cascade depth, size, breadth, structural virality | true-false-news | You need a defensible definition of "spread" for published or client work. |
| Making a most-shared list | what-makes-content-viral | You are studying transmission psychology, with the sampling bias stated. |
| Half-life and curve shape | selfish-gene | You are forecasting duration rather than reach. |

Pick this before picking a model. Most bad virality arguments are two outcome variables in one sentence.
