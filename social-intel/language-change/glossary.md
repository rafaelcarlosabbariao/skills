# Glossary: language change

Every term on the shelf. Slug in parentheses is the chapter to open for the full treatment; this shelf currently has one, so all entries trace to `algospeak`. Where the book uses a term differently from another shelf in The Library, the divergence is noted.

**Affectation** (algospeak): a consciously acquired accent, traditionally distinguished from a "natural" one. Aleksic argues the distinction is weak — sustained affectation becomes accent — and treats it as a gradient. See *influencer accent*.

**African American English (AAE)** (algospeak): the dialect the book identifies as the origin of a large share of what gets called "internet slang" or "Gen Z slang" — "cool", "bae", "fam", "cap", "bussin", "it's giving", "not you", "the way you X", "gyat", "ahh", "opp", "no because". Its words are stigmatised as incorrect and simultaneously sought after as prestigious counterculture, which is what drives the appropriation cycle.

**Algorithmic anxiety** (algospeak; from Chayka, *Filterworld*): the felt loss of control online. Aleksic's refinement: the anxiety is not about the algorithm itself but about the emergent effects it stands for — censorship, surveillance, attention loss, deindividuation, polarisation.

**Algospeak** (algospeak): narrowly, evasive vocabulary invented to avoid algorithmic moderation ("unalive", "seggs", "le$bian"). Aleksic's widened definition, and the shelf's operating one: the entire sociolect produced by algorithmic selection on language, including trend words, aesthetics labels and attention devices.

**Amelioration** (stopgap; Bate, Psyche): a word's sense moving toward the favourable. A movement in sentiment, in the terms of Baes et al. 2024.

**Attention economy** (algospeak; Simon 1971): attention as a scarce resource allocated among competing stimuli in an information-rich environment. The selection pressure every other mechanism on this shelf runs under.

**Backronym** (algospeak): a false expansion invented after the fact. Urban Dictionary's "gyat" entries — "girl you ate that", "girl your ass thick" — against the actual derivation from an AAE pronunciation of "goddamn". Backronyms are an erasure mechanism, not a curiosity.

**Blaccent** (algospeak): a non-Black speaker's imitation of African American English. Century-old, and characterized throughout by an imbalance in who profits from it.

**Bowdlerisation** (algospeak): respelling an offensive word to get it past a filter or a standard. Named for Thomas Bowdler's family-safe Shakespeare. "f*ck", "b!tch", "@ss", "pen*s".

**Brainrot** (algospeak): both a class of Gen Alpha vocabulary ("rizz", "gyat", "skibidi", "fanum tax", "sigma") and the claim that this vocabulary indicates cognitive decline. Oxford's 2024 Word of the Year. Aleksic's correction is load-bearing: there is no evidence for the second sense, and "skibidi" has the same derivation as "Scooby-Doo".

**Broadening** (stopgap; Bate, Psyche): a word's sense widening to cover more referents or contexts. A movement in breadth.

**Clickbait** (algospeak): attention captured through an enticing and often deceptive promise. Distinguished from *ragebait* by valence, not by mechanism.

**Code-switching** (algospeak): shifting between varieties depending on the setting. Everyone does it; the book's point is that "authenticity" arguments about influencers usually mistake code-switching for deception.

**Communication accommodation** (algospeak): the tendency to adjust speech toward or away from an interlocutor. The psychological substrate under domains of use.

**Context collapse** (algospeak; from Marwick & boyd): speech intended for one audience reaching audiences who process it in a different context and reuse it in a new way. The book's mechanism for how in-group words escape and turn hostile — "acoustic", "neurospicy", "slay", "opp".

**Curiosity gap** (algospeak): the marketing Goldilocks zone in which a title says enough to interest and too little to satisfy. "Snowfall in Sequoia National Park, California."

**Cyberbalkanisation** (algospeak): the splintering of the internet into special-interest groups. More bubbles means more spaces for words to be made.

**Digital blackface** (algospeak): disproportionate use of reaction images and GIFs featuring Black people by non-Black users, because the reactions are read as funnier.

**Digital rubbernecking** (algospeak; Narayanan): engaging with content you dislike. Engagement optimization cannot distinguish it from approval, which is why ragebait thrives.

**Diminutive** (algospeak): a form made to sound smaller, cuter or less intense. "Seggs", "peen", "nip nops", "sewerslide". Diminutives are disproportionately successful in algospeak because silliness is catchy and catchiness is fitness.

**Discourse marker** (algospeak): a phrase managing the flow of conversation without changing truth conditions. "Wait", "hold on", and — the book's case study — "no because", which functions online as a scroll-interrupt.

**Domain of use** (algospeak): the setting a variety belongs to. Algospeak's domain is online; it leaks when speakers are too young to distinguish domains, which is how "unalive" reached classrooms and a museum placard.

**Doomslang** (algospeak; from Montell, *The Age of Magical Overthinking*): dystopian or dissociated vocabulary — "it's over", "bedrotting", "doomscrolling", "cope", "wagecuck". Overlaps heavily with incel vocabulary and predates the mainstream by several years.

**Emphatic prosody** (algospeak): stressing more words than the sentence requires. A floor-holding device: each extra stress is another chance to recapture drifting attention.

**Endurance factor** (algospeak): the reason a word keeps being used once the meme that carried it has ended. Operationally, the count of independent contexts and phrasal templates it appears in.

**Engagement optimization** (algospeak; Narayanan): recommendation systems built to maximize comments, shares, likes and retention. The specific mechanism behind every effect on this shelf. Not "algorithms" in general — an algorithm optimizing something else would produce different language.

**Engagement treadmill** (algospeak): Aleksic's coinage. Content earning engagement causes more creators to make it, causing more engagement, causing more creators. Why a keyword trends because the algorithm decided it would.

**Enshittification** (algospeak; Doctorow 2022): platform decay in three stages — good for users, then good for business customers, then extractive toward both.

**Euphemism treadmill** (algospeak; Pinker): the endless replacement cycle for words denoting offensive things. Aleksic's addition: the platform now performs the pejoration step directly, so the treadmill runs faster without suppressing the topic.

**Familect** (algospeak): a family's private register. Cited to establish that everyone is an early adopter of some vocabulary.

**Fanilect** (algospeak): a fandom's sociolect. The Swiftie fanilect ("red scarf", "invisible string", "Gaylor", 13); the K-pop fanilect (*bias*, *anti*, *aegyo*, *sasaeng*).

**Filter bubble** (algospeak): the personalised feed that filters information toward predicted engagement. On this shelf, a word factory — the boundary conditions that let a community invent vocabulary.

**Flanderization** (algospeak): progressive self-caricature toward whatever traits an audience rewards. Named for Ned Flanders. Applies to creators, to subcultures, and to generational labels.

**Floor holding** (algospeak): the conversation-analysis term for keeping the floor in an interaction. The offline equivalent of retention rate, and what the influencer accent is doing.

**Founder effect, linguistic** (algospeak): the first users of a platform set its linguistic culture, and later users inherit it as the standard. Kardashian uptalk → the 2015 "YouTube voice" → the TikTok influencer accent.

**Gen Z shake** (algospeak): the deliberate jolt when a creator sets the phone down at the start of a video. A visual scroll-interrupt, read afterwards as a generational trait.

**Goodhart's law** (algospeak): once a metric becomes a target it ceases to be a good metric. The book's compact explanation for why feeds fill with content nobody wanted.

**Grawlix** (algospeak): the string of symbols standing in for a swear word in comics — `$#*!`, `@##`. Earliest known 1901. Direct ancestor of caption bowdlerisation.

**Hood irony** (algospeak): a meme genre built on gang-associated respellings and vocabulary — the 🅱️ substitution, "thicc", "succ", "cuh", "vro", "opp" — circulating far outside the communities it came from.

**Influencer accent** (algospeak): a self-taught prestige dialect optimized for retention. Three variants: **lifestyle** (uptalk, vowel lengthening, lengthy pauses — the most stereotyped, because it is used by young women), **entertainment** (MrBeast; maximal enthusiasm, ~200 wpm, low variance), **educational** (fast, high pitch variation, scripted).

**Innovation-decision process** (algospeak; Rogers): adoption as two decisions, not one — begin using, then continue or abandon. The second decision is where obtrusiveness kills.

**Leetspeak** (algospeak): the 1980s–90s respelling dialect (`5U1C1D3`, `pr0n`, `413x !z 4 b0j`). The direct precedent for algospeak, and a cautionary case: it died because the population that made it exclusive was overwhelmed, at which point it read as cringe.

**Long tail** (algospeak; Anderson 2004): selling small quantities of many niche items. The economics that microlabels serve, and the reason platforms manufacture the identities they then advertise against.

**Matthew effect** (algospeak): small advantages in attention compound into exponential differences in reach. A post shared 20% less reaches roughly 90% fewer people.

**Meme template / phrasal template** (algospeak): a repeatable verbal or visual formula into which any word can be inserted — "on X", "it's giving X", "in my X era", "she X on my Y till I Z", "X ahh". The unit of endurance.

**Metonymy** (algospeak): referring to something through a closely associated idea. "Red zone" for sexual assault; "the White House" for the executive branch. A common evasion strategy because it is unfilterable.

**Microlabel** (algospeak): a hyper-specific identity term — "goblincore", "pastel goth", "coastal cowgirl". 150+ "-core" variants catalogued; 6,000+ Spotify microgenres. Functionally metadata.

**Millennial pause** (algospeak): the half-second of silence before a millennial starts speaking on camera. Read as a generational trait; actually an artefact of not having been conditioned to eliminate dead air.

**Minced oath** (algospeak): a euphemism made by altering the sound or spelling of an offensive word. "Heck", "gosh", "fug" (Mailer, 1948), "seggs", "Let's Go Brandon".

**Narrowing** (stopgap; Bate, Psyche): a word's sense contracting to fewer referents or contexts. A movement in breadth.

**Obtrusiveness** (algospeak): how noticeable or forced a word feels in use. The strongest single death predictor on this shelf. Field test: if someone can make fun of you for saying it, it is obtrusive.

**Online disinhibition effect** (algospeak): anonymity and distance lowering the cost of hostility. The mechanism behind the "Oxford study" harassment meme.

**Optimal distinctiveness** (algospeak): the balance between needing to belong and needing to be distinct. Microlabels exploit it precisely — enough options to feel unique, one suffix so nobody actually is.

**Pejoration** (stopgap; Bate, Psyche): a word's sense moving toward the unfavourable. A movement in sentiment.

**Poe's law** (algospeak): any sincere expression of extreme views can be read as satire, and any satire as sincere. On this shelf a transmission mechanism: both readings generate engagement, so edgy material travels either way.

**Prestige dialect** (algospeak): a variety associated with a desirable social class. Received Pronunciation, General American, and now the influencer accents — which are unusual in being self-taught rather than propagated by an elite.

**Priming** (algospeak): prior exposure making a related stimulus easier to process. Why "no because" works: audiences have been conditioned to expect something interesting after it.

**Ragebait** (algospeak): attention captured by provoking anger. Precedent: yellow journalism, late nineteenth century.

**Revealed preference** (algospeak): what a person actually engages with, as against **stated preference**, what they say they want to see. Engagement optimization targets the former; the gap between them is widening and is itself a finding.

**Scunthorpe problem** (algospeak): a filter blocking innocent strings containing banned substrings. The Charles Dickens Museum's 2022 shadowban is the book's case.

**Semantic drift** (algospeak): ordinary meaning change over time. "Preppy" moving from prep-school style to bright pink girly clothing inside a decade, accelerated by boutiques optimizing for the hashtag.

**Semantic gap** (algospeak): a concept with no existing word, or no sufficiently good one. The presence of a gap is what holds a word in place after its originating joke ends. "Cancel" filled one; the chair emoji did not.

**Sociolect** (algospeak): a language variety used by a particular social group. The frat house, the family, the fandom, the feed.

**Thought-terminating cliché** (algospeak): a loaded phrase that shuts off dissonant thinking. "It's over", "cope", "AWALT". Cult-linguistics apparatus, documented here in incel forums.

**Trendbait** (algospeak): saying something specifically in the hope it becomes a viral trend. "Girl dinner", "Roman Empire", "beige flag", "orange peel theory", "boomer ellipses".

**Uptalk** (algospeak): rising terminal intonation making a statement sound like a question. Online it functions as an unfinished-sentence signal — the listener waits for a resolution that keeps being deferred.

**Voldemorting** (algospeak; van der Nagel): deliberately avoiding a keyword so content does not surface under it. Originally search-engine avoidance; now also algorithmic, and often expressive — treating a name as too filthy to utter is a political act.

**Whac-A-Mole, linguistic** (algospeak): the ban-and-substitute cycle between moderators and users. Formally unwinnable; a 2015 Georgia Tech study found that flagging every Chinese homophone substitution would false-positive about 20 percent of all posts.
