# Patterns: language change

Reusable procedures assembled from this shelf. Each is stated as name, when to use, steps, and source. Run them in roughly this order when working a term cold: trace the origin, score survival, place it on the gradient, then decide what to report.

---

## 1. The origin trace

**When to use.** First move on any term, aesthetic label, or "Gen Z slang" claim. Mandatory before writing any provenance sentence, and before any emergence claim about vocabulary.

**Steps.**
1. Reject the popular attribution outright. "Gen Z slang", "TikTok slang", "brainrot" are labels applied by out-groups and are never origins.
2. Name the **in-group with the pressing need**. Ask: who had something to say that existing words would not carry, or would get them punished for saying? Every case in the book has one — the mental-health community under moderation, 1980s ball houses, 4chan boards, K-pop and Swiftie fandoms, Chicago rap.
3. Apply the rule of thumb as a prior, not a conclusion: if it did not come from 4chan, it probably came from African American English. Then go looking for the evidence either way.
4. Trace the **platform hops**, not just the years. The incel pipeline is 4chan → Reddit (r/Incels, then r/RateMe) → Twitter/Instagram memes → TikTok/Reels. "Seggs" is 4chan 2013 → Twitter → TikTok 2020. The hop list is the finding; a single origin date is not.
5. Check for a **backronym**. If Urban Dictionary offers an expansion ("girl your ass thick"), assume erasure has already happened and that the real derivation is older and elsewhere.
6. Record what you could not establish. Aleksic himself cannot always prove the direction of causation, and neither can you.

**Source.** algospeak, chs. 1, 5, 6, 7.

## 2. The survival read

**When to use.** Whenever anyone asks how long a term has, or whether a spike is a fad or a shift. Standard input to any forecast half-life band and any term-volume reading.

**Steps.**
1. **Semantic gap — yes or no.** Is there a concept with no adequate existing word? "It is funnier than the alternative" counts. If no gap, stop: forecast a fad and say so. This is the chair-emoji test.
2. **Obtrusiveness — score it.** How often is the word the *subject* of a post rather than the *medium* of one? High meta-commentary means high obtrusiveness. Secondary signals: it appears on published "slang lists"; it has been banned in a classroom; adults perform it ironically; a journalist has explained it.
3. **Endurance — count the frames.** Enumerate the independent phrasal templates and grammatical positions the term occupies. One is fatal. Three or more means it outlives its originating meme.
4. **Frequency — measure it, then discount it.** Volume is the least informative of the four and the most quoted. Record it, do not lead with it.
5. **Combine.** Gap present + unobtrusive + three or more frames is the survival profile. Gap absent + obtrusive + one frame is the death profile. Mixed profiles get a band, not a point.
6. Write the estimate with the score, never on its own.

**Source.** algospeak, ch. 2.

## 3. The substitution-rate check

**When to use.** Any time a topic appears to have gone quiet in a corpus, any THE UNSAID reading, and any claim that moderation reduced discussion of something.

**Steps.**
1. Before concluding decline, list the **known substitutes** for the concept and re-run the count including them. "Suicide" requires "unalive", "un@l!ve", "sewerslide", "kermit sewerslide", "ropemaxx" at minimum. (Corrected 2026-09-23: this list also carried "LDAR", which the book glosses on p. 136 as incel doomslang for "lay down and rot", not as a suicide code. Steen, Yurechko & Klug 2023, Table 1, p. 5, is the second source for this family.)
2. Compute **substitution rate**: substitutes as a share of total mentions of the concept. Rising substitution with flat or rising total means suppression, not decline.
3. Rising substitution with *falling* total is the ambiguous case. Say so; do not pick.
4. Expect the substitute set to **churn faster than the concept**. Budget the harvest for new substitutes rather than assuming the list is stable.
5. Look for the **metalinguistic tell**: creators winking at the constraint (an unrelated opening before a bait-and-switch, an emoji standing in for a flag). That is direct evidence of a live constraint.
6. Never report "the conversation moved on" without having run steps 1–3.

**Source.** algospeak, chs. 1, 10.

## 4. Appropriation-gradient placement

**When to use.** Whenever a term is being read as evidence about a community, and in any WHO'S TALKING mapping. Also before quoting a term in client-facing work.

**Steps.**
1. Place the term on the gradient: **innovator in-group → adjacent group seeking its prestige → peripheral circles → mainstream**. Ball slang runs houses → drag → gay → straight women → middle schoolers.
2. Ask **who can still correct misuse**. If the origin community has no correction channel to the current users, the term has left their hands and its meaning is now unstable.
3. Assume **attribution has decayed geometrically**. At the observed rates most current users do not know the origin, and >85% of Aleksic's surveyed adults believe children do not know the AAE derivation.
4. Determine the **terminal state**: discarded as passé ("fleek") or normalized as standard ("cool"). Both are losses for the origin community; naming which one is the finding.
5. If your corpus is sampling the mainstream end, **say which end you sampled**. A read on "slay" from a general TikTok corpus is not a read on ball culture.
6. Never treat mainstream usage as evidence of the origin community's current position.

**Source.** algospeak, ch. 7.

## 5. The irony-dose split

**When to use.** Any term arriving with an ironic frame, any manosphere or extremist vocabulary in a corpus, and any read where "it's just a joke" is offered as a reason not to worry.

**Steps.**
1. State the two readings explicitly: the **ironic dose** and the **sincere dose**. They are carried on the same object.
2. Assume both are being received. Poe's law is symmetric: sincere-read-as-joke earns likes, ironic-read-as-sincere earns outrage, and the feed rewards both.
3. Estimate the **sincere fraction** from downstream behavior, not from tone. The book's evidence is behavioral: canthal-tilt eyeliner tutorials and jaw-surgery searches rose after the mewing jokes went viral, and incel forums coined "Tiktokcel" for the influx.
4. Check whether the **joke is load-bearing for the spread**. If the term only travels because it is funny, expect the sincere fraction to grow as the joke ages and the framing is stripped.
5. Report the harm against the sincere fraction, and the reach against the ironic one. Reporting only one is the standard error.

**Source.** algospeak, ch. 6.

## 6. Metadata-label decode

**When to use.** Any named aesthetic, microgenre, or "-core"; any brief that treats a subculture as a demographic; any question about whether a scene is real.

**Steps.**
1. Ask whether the label **preceded or followed the community**. "Pastel goth" and "cottagecore" existed on Tumblr before short-form video; "preppy" was recaptured by boutiques optimizing a hashtag. Both directions occur, and they mean different things.
2. Test whether the label is doing **discovery work or targeting work**. If creators use it because it gets recommended rather than because it describes, it is metadata wearing an aesthetic's clothes.
3. Look for the **commercial tie**: a shop, a haul, an integrated storefront. TikTok's own framing is "subcultures are the new demographics".
4. Check for **precision loss**. Sophie of `cutandcropped` knew "maximalist" was the accurate word and used "preppy" because it performed. Expect the trending label to displace the accurate one, and note what was lost.
5. Ask what **rules the label imports** (Walton). Each category constrains what counts as belonging to it, so a proliferation of labels is a contraction of expressive room, not an expansion.

**Source.** algospeak, ch. 8.

## 7. Loop discipline

**When to use.** Any time a finding is about to be written as "the algorithm caused X" or "the algorithm just reflects X". Applies to every arm.

**Steps.**
1. Refuse both framings. Narayanan's position, and it matches a Latourian discipline against invoking forces.
2. Name the three legs: the **human need**, the **platform incentive**, and the **return leg** by which the platform's output changes the human behavior.
3. If you cannot name all three with evidence, say the loop is untraced. Do not substitute a direction.
4. Name the **optimization target** specifically. "Engagement optimization" is a claim you can check; "the algorithm" is not.
5. Where the loop is traced, state the emergent outcome as belonging to the system rather than to either side.

**Source.** algospeak, ch. 5.
