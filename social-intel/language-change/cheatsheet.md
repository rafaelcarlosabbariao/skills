# Cheatsheet: language change

Fast lookups. Every row traces to `chapters/algospeak.md`. Nothing here substitutes for the chapter when the call is consequential — and this shelf is one work deep, so treat everything below as a well-argued hypothesis, not a settled result.

---

## 1. Situation to model

| Situation | Which model applies | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A term is spiking and you must call fad or entry | Four survival factors | Semantic gap first. No gap, no entry — everything else is amplitude. |
| You need a half-life for a term | Obtrusiveness plus endurance | Meta-commentary rate, then count of independent phrasal templates. Never lead with volume. |
| A topic looks like it went quiet | Whac-A-Mole plus substitution rate | Re-run the count with the known substitutes included before writing "declined". |
| A term is being called "Gen Z slang" | Origin trace | Which in-group had the pressing need. The label is an out-group artefact, never an origin. |
| An in-group word is reaching outsiders | Context collapse plus appropriation gradient | Whether the origin community can still correct misuse. If not, forecast pejoration. |
| A word is used ironically by one crowd, sincerely by another | Poe's law, irony-dose split | Downstream behavior, not tone. Search and purchase trails reveal the sincere fraction. |
| A new aesthetic label appears | Metadata-label decode | Whether the label preceded the community or was minted to be targeted against. |
| Everyone in a niche suddenly sounds identical | Linguistic founder effect plus prestige dialect | Who the first users were and whom they were copying. |
| Someone says the algorithm caused a language change | Emergent human-algorithm effects | Name all three legs of the loop or declare it untraced. |
| A metric is offered as proof of what people want | Goodhart plus stated/revealed preferences | Say which preference you are reporting. They diverge, and the gap is the story. |
| A term has an obvious meaning and an obvious spelling | Backronym check | Assume erasure. The real derivation is usually older, elsewhere, and stigmatised. |
| Engagement is high and sentiment is hostile | Digital rubbernecking | Do not read engagement as approval. The feed cannot tell them apart, and neither should you. |
| A brand or politician adopts a trending term | Flanderization plus the summer-label pattern | Whether tying the thing to a moment also ties it to the moment's expiry. |
| A community's search term is blocked | Whac-A-Mole | Find the substitute before concluding the community left the platform. |

## 2. Obtrusiveness scoring

Rough five-point scale. Higher is more obtrusive and shorter-lived.

| Score | Signal | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | The word *is* the joke. Cannot be used sincerely. | chair emoji 🪑, "le$bian" / "le dollar bean" |
| 4 | Appears on published slang lists; adults perform it ironically; explained by a newspaper. | "skibidi", "gyat", "rizz", "fanum tax" |
| 3 | Marked as belonging to a generation or platform, but usable straight. | "yeet", "on fleek", "delulu", "slay" (current) |
| 2 | Familiar words in a new sense; occasionally remarked on. | "function", "opp", "it's giving", "in my X era" |
| 1 | Entered unnoticed. No meta-commentary. | "cancel", "side-eye", "unalive" (in its own domain) |

**The field test.** If someone can make fun of you for saying it, it is 3 or above. **The compilation effect:** publishing a list of a term shifts it upward. A slang list is a leading indicator of death, and writing one is a small cause of it.

## 3. Half-life expectations

Directional only. The book fits no curves and neither should you without doing the work.

| Gap | Obtrusiveness | Frames | Expected outcome | Case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None | 5 | 1 | Weeks. Dies with the joke. | chair emoji: viral Sept 2021, gone by Nov |
| None | 4 | 2–3 | Months to ~2 years. Cycles through the treadmill until meaningless. | "gyat", "skibidi" |
| Real | 4 | 3+ | Survives the meme, then decays as laggards arrive. Reclaimed ironically. | "sigma", "-maxxing", "pilled" |

## 4. The four factors, as questions

1. **Gap** — what does this do that an existing word does not? ("It is funnier" is a real answer.)
2. **Obtrusiveness** — is the word the subject or the medium?
3. **Endurance** — how many independent frames does it live in?
4. **Frequency** — how much, and against what baseline? Record last, quote least.

## 6. Standing corrections

- **A slang list is a death certificate.** Compiling it raises obtrusiveness.
- **Suppression moves the word, not the conversation.** Falling counts for a term are not falling interest in the topic.
- **The generation is not the origin.** Ever.
- **Novelty is not harm.** "Skibidi" and "Scooby-Doo" have the same derivation.
- **Engagement is not approval.** Rubbernecking looks identical in the metrics.
- **Irony is not a safe dose.** Some fraction always receives it straight.
- **More labels is less freedom.** Each category arrives with rules attached.
