# Social Psychology Shelf

Six works, one working model of how cultural behavior gets produced. The shelf exists so that a trend read, an audience rule set, or a forecast ensemble starts from mechanism instead of vibes. Every chapter file on this shelf ends in decision rules; this file is the layer above them: what the works agree on, where they fight, and which one to reach for.

## The unified mental model

**1. Behavior is automatic before it is deliberate.** Five of the six works converge on the same architecture from different directions. Cialdini's "click, whirr": a single trigger feature releases a whole compliance sequence. Kahneman's System 1: fast, associative, always on, with System 2 mostly rubber-stamping its output. Haidt's elephant and rider: the affective verdict arrives first and conscious reasoning decorates it afterward. Levitin's schemas: exposure builds a predictive model, and feeling is generated by how events confirm or violate its predictions. Van Bavel and Packer add that identity filters perception before reasoning even starts. The shared consequence for this practice: when a trend spreads, the honest first question is never "what argument won" but "what cue fired, in which fast system, for whom."

**2. Cues substitute for evidence, so cues can be counterfeited.** The fast system keys on a small number of features: a reason-shaped sentence, popularity metrics, credential aesthetics, repetition, schema fit, an in-group marker. Each substitution is normally adaptive and each is exploitable the moment the cue is cheap to fake. This is the shelf's sharpest lens on the slop pressure system: AI slop is industrialized cue-counterfeiting. Fake engagement forges social proof (Cialdini), optimized fluency passes System 1 with zero System 2 engagement (Kahneman), and schema-perfect output with no deliberate expectation violation is the machine signature (Levitin). The three tests stack into one pass: whose proof, how easy, how surprising.

**3. Belonging outranks accuracy.** Van Bavel and Packer's identity-based model of belief makes it explicit: belief formation weighs accuracy against belonging, status, and moral goals, and belonging routinely wins, which is why much stated "belief" is expressive cheering. Haidt supplies the deeper claim: morality evolved to bind teams, and the binding is what makes the other team illegible. Cialdini's social proof and liking are the compliance-level face of the same force. Operationally: any stated position collected under high identity salience is a loyalty signal until proven otherwise, and the corrective is stakes, not facts (small accuracy incentives shrink partisan factual gaps by roughly half).

**4. Structure produces behavior; read the game before reading character.** Axelrod is the shelf's cold-water work. Cooperation and defection are outputs of interaction structure, chiefly the shadow of the future w, not of goodwill: WWI trench units cooperated with the enemy because the same units met repeatedly, and dating apps produce ghosting because they make interactions effectively one-shot. Before diagnosing a scene's values, estimate its w, its payoffs, and its recognition of past partners. This "behavior as emergent output of local rules" stance is the theoretical spine of audience simulation, and Kahneman's reference dependence extends it: agents respond to changes from a reference point, with losses weighted about 2.25x gains, not to absolute states.

**5. Feeling is managed prediction error.** Levitin's expectation-violation model, though written about music, generalizes across the eight domains: exposure builds a schema, deviation generates tension, resolution releases reward, and liking follows an inverted-U over familiarity. Zero deviation is forgettable, extreme deviation is noise. This gives the trend read a content-level instrument that the other works lack: not who is spreading a thing or why they say they like it, but whether the thing itself carries the signature of a human choice.

Stacked, the model reads: a cultural event is a cue firing (Cialdini) inside a fast judgment system (Kahneman), filtered by whichever identity is currently salient (Van Bavel and Packer), scored against a moral matrix (Haidt), inside an interaction structure that sets the payoff for cooperating or defecting (Axelrod), and felt as expectation confirmed or violated (Levitin). A full read touches all six layers; most daily reads need two or three.

## Where the works disagree

**Familiarity: monotone or inverted-U?** Kahneman's fluency findings say repetition raises liking (mere exposure): the more often it is seen, the truer and better it feels. Levitin says liking peaks at intermediate familiarity and then falls to exhaustion. The reconciliation is that mere exposure describes the ascending limb of Levitin's curve, but the practical stakes are real: Kahneman predicts feeds that repeat content manufacture durable preference; Levitin predicts they burn it out. For the taste pressure system, track exposure position: early-curve liking spikes are fluency artifacts, late-curve fatigue is saturation, and neither is a taste shift.

**What a fight is.** The shelf offers three rival diagnoses of the same conflict. Haidt: a values conflict, two moral matrices weighting the six foundations differently, so neither side can hear the other. Van Bavel and Packer: an identity conflict that needs no substantive disagreement at all, since minimal cues alone produce in-group favoritism, and most discrimination is in-group love rather than out-group hate. Axelrod: a payoff feud, two provocable unforgiving strategies locked in an echo of alternating retaliation. The diagnoses matter because the fixes diverge: a values conflict needs reframing into the other matrix, favoritism-driven conflict responds to superordinate identity, and an echo needs a forgiveness mechanic. Prescribing across diagnoses fails. Triage first (see patterns.md).

**What an agent is.** Axelrod's agents are rational payoff-maximizers, and he still gets cooperation out of them, which is a warning against over-ascribing values to behavior that structure explains. Kahneman's agents are systematically biased. Van Bavel's and Haidt's agents are belonging-driven. Audience simulations must choose per question: use Axelrod agents to test strategy and structure, Kahneman agents to test framing and dose, identity agents to test mobilization and containment. Mixing all three into one undifferentiated "realistic agent" produces an untestable blob.

**Do stated reasons matter?** Haidt says reasons are post-hoc decorations that never carried the verdict. Cialdini's Langer study says even an empty "because I have to make copies" raises compliance from 60 to 93 percent. Both agree reasons do not persuade on their merits; they disagree on whether uttering one changes anything. Resolution for practice: a reason works as a trigger feature, not as an argument, so count reason-shaped language as a cue, never as evidence of deliberation.

**Whose intuition to trust.** Kahneman restricts trustworthy intuition to high-validity environments with regular structure and fast feedback, and notes culture reading is not one by default. Yet Cialdini's compliance professionals and Haidt's elephants show intuition dominating everywhere. The asymmetry is the point: your subjects run on intuition and that is the phenomenon; you, the analyst, do not get to. The audience's System 1 is the data; the analyst's System 1 is the hazard. Hence the shelf's standing analyst disciplines: outside view first, availability checked against base rates, confidence discounted in proportion to narrative coherence.

**One replication caveat.** The Kahneman chapter flags that the social-priming material has replicated poorly; keep the fluency and heuristics core, hold behavioral priming loosely. Haidt's foundation profiles come from WEIRD-skewed online samples; use the slopes, not the absolute scores.

## Which work is the right tool

- **A surge is moving and you need to know why now:** Cialdini for the lever, Kahneman for whether the size is an availability artifact.
- **A judgment, forecast, or metric read is being made:** Kahneman. Base rates, regression, framing, premortems.
- **A "we" is forming, a label is spreading, stated beliefs look weird:** Van Bavel and Packer.
- **Two camps cannot hear each other, purity language is out, framings keep failing:** Haidt.
- **Trust is decaying, a feud will not die, a platform's incentives are producing bad behavior:** Axelrod.
- **The object itself needs reading (is it slop, is it new, who is the revival for):** Levitin.
