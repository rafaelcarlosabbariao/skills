# The Righteous Mind

## The claim

Moral judgment is intuition first, strategic reasoning second: the rider (conscious reasoning) mostly serves the elephant (automatic affect) that has already decided. Tribes differ because their intuitions weight six moral foundations differently, so culture-war opponents are not reasoning badly about one shared morality, they are running different moralities. Morality binds and blinds: it evolved to bind people into teams, and the binding is what makes the other team illegible.

## Core claims

- Intuitions come first, reasoning second. In moral dumbfounding studies (the Julie and Mark harmless-incest vignette), subjects keep condemning after every stated reason is refuted: the verdict is intuitive, the reasons are decorations.
- Moral reasoning is Glauconian, built for reputation rather than truth, a press secretary. Evidence standards flip with motivation: "Can I believe it?" for welcome conclusions, "Must I believe it?" for unwelcome ones.
- Morality is wider than harm and fairness. WEIRD populations (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic; Henrich et al. 2010) are global outliers; their harm-and-rights morality is the exception, not the human baseline.
- Six foundations structure moral intuition: Care/harm, Fairness/cheating (as proportionality, not equality), Loyalty/betrayal, Authority/subversion, Sanctity/degradation, Liberty/oppression. YourMorals.org data (Graham, Haidt, and Nosek 2009, JPSP): liberals load on Care and Fairness and decline steeply on Loyalty, Authority, and Sanctity moving left; conservatives score all six roughly equally.
- The illegibility is asymmetric: conservatives and moderates predicted liberals' moral answers better than liberals predicted conservatives' (Graham, Nosek, and Haidt 2012); the most liberal respondents were least accurate.
- Humans are "90 percent chimp, 10 percent bee": individual competitors with a groupish overlay from multilevel selection. The hive switch (synchrony, ritual, awe, oxytocin) temporarily dissolves self into group.

## Frameworks and models

**Social Intuitionist Model (SIM).** From Haidt's 2001 Psychological Review paper. Six links: (1) intuitive judgment, an affective flash produces the verdict; (2) post-hoc reasoning; (3) reasoned persuasion, my arguments work on your intuitions, not your logic; (4) social persuasion, group opinion shifts intuitions directly. Links 5 and 6, reasoned judgment and private reflection, exist but are rare. Practical reading: persuasion is elephant-to-elephant, carried by messengers, identity, and affect.

**Moral Foundations Theory (MFT).** Six evolved "taste receptors," each with an adaptive challenge, an original trigger, and expandable current triggers (Sanctity: pathogen avoidance, originally corpses and waste, now anything coded contaminated). Measured by the 30-item Moral Foundations Questionnaire: two 15-item halves (relevance, judgment), 0 to 5 per item, averaged into six subscale scores. The operative parameters are a group's foundation profile (a six-weight vector) and the ideology slopes above; the profile predicts which framings land and which register as noise.

**Moral matrix.** The consensual web of values, practices, and institutions that makes a community's morality self-evident inside and perverse outside. Map the matrix before judging the behavior.

**Hive switch and moral capital.** Multilevel selection gives humans conditional hivishness, flipped on by synchrony, ritual, and awe. Moral capital is the stock of norms and institutions that sustains cooperation; changes that spend it on Care grounds can degrade the group even while helping individuals.

## Decision rules

- If two audiences fight over "facts" and neither updates, then code each side's language by foundation and file it as a values conflict, not an information gap. Debunking will not move it.
- If you need to move an audience, then lead with the elephant: identity-congruent messengers and intuition first, reasons second.
- If purity and contamination vocabulary appears (clean, raw, natural, toxic, slop), then treat Sanctity as active even in secular scenes; expect disgust-driven virality and hard in/out boundaries.
- If a judgment survives refutation of all its stated reasons, then it is a taboo. Forecast persistence.
- If messaging loads only Care and Fairness, then expect underperformance with conservative-leaning and non-WEIRD segments; widen the foundation mix to widen reach.
- If you see synchronized collective ritual (chants, dances, meme lockstep), then expect hive effects: spiking loyalty, suppressed dissent, short half-life unless institutionalized.
- If engagement runs high on a claim a community wants true, then apply the "Can I believe it?" discount before reading it as conviction.

## Key terms

- **Social Intuitionist Model:** judgment from instant intuition; reasoning as post-hoc justification; change flows through social channels.
- **Elephant and rider:** automatic intuition versus conscious reasoning; the rider serves the elephant.
- **Moral foundations:** six evolved intuitive systems (Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity, Liberty) weighted differently across groups.
- **Moral dumbfounding:** holding a moral verdict after all supporting reasons are refuted.
- **Moral matrix:** the shared value-and-institution web that makes a group's morality self-evident inside and opaque outside.
- **WEIRD:** Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic samples; psychological outliers overrepresented in research.
- **Glauconian reasoning:** reasoning optimized for reputation, not truth.
- **Hive switch:** the capacity, triggered by synchrony and awe, to shift from self-interest to group absorption.
- **Moral capital:** the norm-and-institution stock that sustains a community's cooperation.
