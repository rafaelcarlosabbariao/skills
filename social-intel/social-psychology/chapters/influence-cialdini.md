# Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion

## The claim

Compliance mostly runs on automatic shortcuts, not deliberation. Six lever principles, reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity, act as trigger features that fire fixed responses, and compliance professionals win by staging the trigger rather than arguing the merits. The shortcuts are usually adaptive; they become exploitable whenever the cue is counterfeited.

## Core claims

- People run "click, whirr" fixed-action patterns: a single trigger feature (a reason, a title, a countdown) releases the whole compliance sequence. Langer's copy-machine study: 60 percent complied with a bare request, 94 percent with a real reason, 93 percent with the empty "because I have to make copies."
- Reciprocity obligates even when the gift is unwanted, and it powers concession trades. Rejection-then-retreat: 17 percent agreed to chaperone a zoo trip when asked cold, 50 percent after first refusing a larger ask (Cialdini et al., 1975).
- Small commitments restructure self-image and compound. Freedman and Fraser: 76 percent accepted an ugly "Drive Carefully" billboard after a trivial prior commitment, versus 17 percent cold. Commitments bind hardest when active, public, effortful, and seen as freely chosen.
- Social proof dominates under two amplifiers, uncertainty and similarity. It explains canned laughter, bystander inaction (pluralistic ignorance), and copycat effects such as Phillips' Werther-effect suicide data.
- Authority is read from symbols, titles, clothing, trappings, not substance. Milgram: about 65 percent of subjects delivered the maximum 450-volt shock on an experimenter's say-so.
- Liking flows from attractiveness, similarity, compliments, cooperation, and mere association; scarcity works through loss framing and reactance, and bites hardest when something is newly scarce and competed for (Worchel's cookie-jar experiments).

## Frameworks and models

**The six weapons of influence.** Each is a heuristic that substitutes a cue for evidence: reciprocity (obligation from receiving), commitment/consistency (align with prior acts), social proof (others' behavior defines correct behavior), authority (defer to credentialed sources), liking (agree with people you like), scarcity (rare equals valuable). No formal math; the operative parameters are compliance-rate deltas between cue-present and cue-absent conditions, roughly two to four times baseline in the anchor studies above.

**Trigger features and judgmental heuristics.** Borrowed from ethology (Tinbergen's mother turkeys responding to a "cheep" from a stuffed predator): responses key on one feature of the situation, not the whole. "Expensive equals good" is the human version.

**The contrast principle.** Judgments are relative to what preceded them. Show the costly item first and the mid-priced item reads cheap. This is the staging layer underneath door-in-the-face and real-estate "setup" properties.

**Amplifier conditions.** Social proof scales with uncertainty and with similarity of the modeled others. Commitment scales with active, public, effortful, uncoerced acts. Scarcity scales with recency of the scarcity and visible rivalry. These conditionals, not the raw principles, are what make the framework predictive.

## Decision rules

- If a trend's evidence is mostly popularity metrics (views, "everyone is doing X"), then classify it as social-proof-driven and check whether the proof is manufactured before calling it organic demand.
- If scarcity is staged (drops, waitlists, countdowns), then expect a demand spike plus reversal once supply normalizes; do not read the spike as a taste shift.
- If an audience has made public identity commitments (bio labels, pledges, purchases), then expect consistency pressure to beat counterevidence; persuasion must reframe the commitment, not attack it.
- If uncertainty is high and actors resemble each other, then expect herding and pluralistic ignorance; discount early consensus.
- If content is being restricted or banned, then expect reactance to raise its perceived value; censorship reads as amplification.
- If authority cues are purely aesthetic (credentials in bio, lab-coat styling, confident jargon), then verify substance; these cues are now cheap to counterfeit.

## Key terms

- **click, whirr**: automatic fixed-action compliance released by a single trigger feature.
- **reciprocity rule**: obligation to repay what another provides, even unrequested.
- **rejection-then-retreat**: large ask, refusal, smaller target ask; refusal reads as a concession to reciprocate.
- **foot-in-the-door**: small commitment that shifts self-image and enables larger compliance.
- **social proof**: using others' behavior to define correct behavior, strongest under uncertainty and similarity.
- **pluralistic ignorance**: everyone scans everyone else's calm and concludes nothing is wrong.
- **reactance**: motivational push to reclaim a freedom when it is restricted; scarcity's engine.
- **contrast principle**: the same option reads differently depending on what came just before it.
