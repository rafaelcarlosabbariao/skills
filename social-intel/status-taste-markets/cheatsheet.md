## Situation table

| Situation | Which model applies | Check first |
|---|---|---|
| A chart-topper, viral hit, or runaway leaderboard item | MusicLab | Convergence across independent surfaces, and any independent-appeal proxy. Rank is frozen early accident plus a quality bound |
| Trend spiking in one community or one trend domain | Diverse Local Trends | Sibling communities and overall volume in the same run. One world is a finding, all worlds is a shift |
| A scene sneering at "the mainstream" or "the algorithm" | Club Cultures | Map the disgust boundary; this is identity work, not a description of a real audience. Note what gets feminized |
| Mass media condemning a scene (moral panic) | Club Cultures | Forecast growth and hardened insider identity, not decline. Tag the media tier before scoring |
| Mass media warmly embracing a scene | Club Cultures + Status and Culture | Forecast capital flight, elite abandonment, and forking into new micro-scenes |
| Aesthetic crossing from its originating tier into mass adoption | Status and Culture | Cycle stage: originator tier, current holders, signaling-cost trajectory. Scan originators for the replacement |
| Two structurally similar audiences into different things | Wide Social Influence + Diverse Local Trends | Attribute to path-dependent influence first; do not hunt for preference differences. Compare embedded trend regions, not item lists |
| Adoption in a community with no preference basis there | Wide Social Influence | Trace the partially similar bridge communities feeding it before theorizing a taste shift |
| Platform adds ranked feed, trending tab, or visible counts | MusicLab + Diverse Local Trends | Expect inequality and unpredictability to rise together with signal salience; discount early leaderboard positions |
| Platform hides or removes popularity signals | MusicLab + Diverse Local Trends | Expect flatter distributions tracking quality more closely; predictions get more stable |
| Recommender tightening exposure toward high-similarity peers | Wide Social Influence | Expect fewer unexpected hits; watch group chats, curators, and scenes for reintroduced surprise |
| AI slop winning share under algorithmic ranking | MusicLab + Status and Culture | Two ledgers: slop above the quality floor can win share, but zero-cost signals carry no cachet. Watch human-cost counter-signals (handmade, live, slow) gaining rank |
| Insiders policing sell-outs | Club Cultures + Status and Culture | Capital near peak valuation; crossover close; early holders about to exit into new undergrounds |
| Elite actors going quiet or understated | Status and Culture | Read counter-signaling, not disengagement |
| A group's conventions look strange or irrational | Status and Culture | Alternative hierarchy with inverted values; read strange conventions as status moves, not noise |
| Someone explains a taste as pure preference | Status and Culture + Club Cultures | The status taboo. Ask which status strategy the taste serves before asking if it is sincere |
| Asked to predict which item wins a cultural market | MusicLab + Wide Social Influence + Diverse Local Trends | Refuse the point prediction. Forecast the distribution: inequality level, top-slot share, spread of plausible winners, floor/ceiling |
| Authenticity dispute (real vs fake forms) | Club Cultures | Authenticity is manufactured and movable (the record went from fake to authentic). Read the scene politics setting it |
| Trend running on pure novelty, no accruing value | Status and Culture | Fad, short half-life. Patina separates classics |
| Dating when a scene "started" | Club Cultures | Date public life from the niche-media naming event, not the practice's origin |

## Quick rules

1. Popularity is never a measurement. Default prior on any winner: cascade until multi-surface convergence proves appeal.
2. Quality screens out losers; it does not pick winners. Bet against bottom-decile appeal, bet against total failure for top-decile, refuse point calls in the middle.
3. Rising volume plus falling signaling cost equals late cycle, regardless of how loud it currently is.
4. Hostile mass attention grows a scene; friendly mass adoption kills its signals. Track scene identity and signal value as separate ledgers.
5. Closest peers are the least informative about novel adoption. Influence peaks at partial homophily; bin by similarity before weighting.
6. Every trend call names the world it is local to.
7. Performed contempt locates a status economy more reliably than stated loves.
8. More salient popularity signals mean more winner-take-all and less predictability, at the same time.
9. Perceived popularity is itself a causal force: false hits partly stick, and quality reasserts only at the extremes over time.
10. Do not write resistance narratives where a capital account will do.
